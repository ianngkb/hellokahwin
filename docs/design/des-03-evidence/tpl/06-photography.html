Photography — three frame classes and their byte ceilings

DES-02 counted the library rather than describing it: of eleven photographs pulled off our own CDN, four hold at any size and seven come apart above roughly 300 px of enlargement. That is not a spectrum to eyeball per photo at build time — it is a class, assigned once, that gates which derivative a cover is allowed to reach. Below is the assignment for all eleven, the ceiling each class is permitted, and what happens at the two edges: a class-G cover, and no cover at all.

The rule this table exists to enforce

Cover class is an editorial selection input, checked at the point an article is written or its cover is chosen — not a rendering decision made silently by whatever crop happens to fit. A class-G photograph is never assigned as an article cover or a homepage hero. If the only candidate photograph for a new article is class G, the article ships with the no-cover layout in §7.2 rather than an enlarged class-G frame. That is a real editorial cost — managing-editor and whoever selects photography need this table before publishing, not after a reader sees a soft image.

Against what production serves today (DES-09 §3.5): the shipped 4:5 mobile cover has a median of 1,405,400 B — DES-09's G19 vetoed it outright — and the shipped 4:3 card a median of 823,997 B. Every ceiling above sits at 3–11% of what ships now, on the same photographs. Exactly one image is preloaded per page (the article or hero cover); everything else is loading=lazy with srcset+sizes, which is also DES-09 G22.

6.3  The two edges: a class-G cover, and no cover

Both are drawn as states in §7.2, not asserted here. In outline: a class-G cover is downgraded to the card ceiling and never enlarged past it, wherever the layout would otherwise ask for a column or hero placement; a missing cover removes the figure entirely and gives its vertical space to the text, rather than rendering a broken <img> or a placeholder box that implies a photograph is coming.
