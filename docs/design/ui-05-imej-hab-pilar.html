Sprint 04 · UI-05 · Track design

Hab pilar kekal tanpa imej

Tujuh daripada 44 halaman kategori tidak memaparkan sebarang gambar. Ketujuh-tujuhnya ialah hab pilar — templat yang berbeza sama sekali. Dokumen ini merekodkan keputusan untuk mengekalkannya tanpa imej, sebab yang boleh dipertahankan dengan ukuran, syarat yang membalikkannya, dan set keadaan penuh bagi templat itu.

Tarikh 31 Ogos 2026 Pemilik product-designer Dipersetujui creative-director Diukur pada hellokahwin.com langsung Lebar 390px, Chrome + playwright-core

1The premise in the ticket is wrong, and the correction narrows the question

UI-05 was written from one measurement: /artikel/hantaran-mas-kahwin renders zero images. That measurement is correct. The generalisation drawn from it — "category pages render zero images" — is not.

I fetched all 44 live category URLs on 31 Ogos 2026 and counted <img> in the served HTML.

URLs fetched44 every category in the nav and the /artikel index
Already carry photography37 1–15 images each
Render zero images7 all seven are pillar hubs
The check was verified before the absence was believed

A grep returning zero is a claim about the regex. I enumerated every tag on the pillar page instead of testing for the one I expected: 56 <a>, 34 <script>, 30 <div>, 13 <svg>, 5 <h2>, 0 <img>, 0 <picture>. A case-insensitive search for img returns exactly two hits, both role="img" on the wordmark SVG. The absence is real.

The seven zero-image pages are not category listings that lost their pictures. They are pillar hubs, which render through src/components/inspire/pillar-body.tsx — a different component, reached by a different branch (if (category.isPillar)), fed by a different query (getPillarView) whose row type PillarClusterArticle carries no image field at all. The other 37 render through CategoryCard / CategoryRow in the same route file, and both of those already emit <img> with srcset.

What this means for the item

UI-05's DoD says "category pages carry imagery … or a written rationale for why they stay text-only." 37 of 44 category pages already carry imagery. The decision this document records is therefore narrower than the DoD's wording assumed: it is about the seven pillar hubs, and nothing in it changes the 37 grid pages.

2The decision

Decided

The seven pillar hubs stay text-only. No thumbnail grid, no per-row thumbnail, no lead card. The cluster map is carried by typography.

Agreed by the creative director on 31 Ogos 2026 after reviewing the rendered evidence (§4).

Rejected: thumbnails on pillar rows

Rejected not on taste but on the asset library, measured: at the real 80px mobile treatment the covers do not form a set, two pairs are the same photograph, and the four highest-value pages are illustrated with buildings.

The reason, in one sentence

The asset library cannot support a thumbnail treatment on these pages today. This is not a claim that topic hubs should never carry photography — that would be a taste claim, and the creative director declined to make it. It is a claim about these assets, at this quality, in this slot. §8 states what reverses it.

Rejected argument — recorded so it is not made later

"Do it for consistency: 37 of 44 category pages have images, so the pillars are the odd ones out." Consistency of template is not a virtue when the pages do different jobs. The 37 are the browse surface — reverse-chronological listings of what exists. A pillar is a map of a topic that deliberately renders sections which do not exist yet (eight of them are live right now). Making the map look like the listing would destroy the distinction the pillar architecture exists to draw.

Rejected argument — and this one is explicitly forbidden

Any SEO justification. Decision 86 measured these pages at ~16 impressions and zero clicks. Nothing in this document is argued from traffic, and nothing in it should be scored on traffic later. This is a brand and credibility decision.

The creative director's reading of the same sheet, which is the part that is their remit and not mine: a thumbnail grid works when the images share a register — one light, one distance to subject, one palette — and this sheet has at least four incompatible registers six-across, so it reads as a scraped image search. And it destroys the palette: the ground is #edeae1 and the system runs near-monochrome on a single bronze accent, #725825; the sheet introduces saturated turquoise, hot pink, magenta, lime and orange at 80px intervals.

The rule a thumbnail grid breaks

A cover must depict its subject. Inside an article a weak cover is one image beside 1,500 words that do the depicting. In a thumbnail grid the image is the entry — nothing else carries it. So the grid promotes precisely the covers that fail that rule into the position where the failure is most visible, and it does it to the state-rate pages, which are the most-searched content on the site. An office block cannot represent "mas kahwin Perak" when it is the only thing in the slot.

3.4 Aspect: the sources do not agree with the slot

True intrinsic dimensions of all 38 covers, read from a detached Image() on the asset URL:

An explanation in an earlier draft of this document was wrong

My first pass read img.naturalWidth off the live page elements and got 176×88 for assets that are genuinely 1200×800. I attributed that to reading mid-decode. That reason was wrong, and UI-03 found the real one: on an element carrying a srcset with w descriptors, naturalWidth returns the intrinsic width divided by the pixel density the browser derived from sizes.

I re-tested rather than adopting the correction on trust. On the live grid page, with every image reporting complete: true and three seconds past decode: sizes="176px" against a 1200px asset gives a density of 1200 ÷ 176 = 6.818, and 1200 ÷ 6.818 = 176. The number I saw, exactly. A detached Image() on the same currentSrc returns 1200×800.

Why the wrong reason mattered more than the wrong number: the conclusion (use a detached Image) and the measurements were right either way, because dims.mjs always loaded assets by URL with no srcset. But a reader following my stated reason would have waited for decode and still got wrong numbers forever. Any upscale check written as boxWidth / img.naturalWidth returns about 1.0 by construction and can never fire — a direct instruction to UI-06's gate.

3.5 The pillar renders empty sections on purpose, and a photograph cannot render a promise

Four empty clusters are live right now across three pillars, each rendering "Artikel untuk … akan datang tidak lama lagi." That is deliberate, documented in pillar-body.tsx: the pillar is the map, and a cluster with nothing under it yet is information for a reader and a commitment for the editorial team. Hiding it would make the pillar look complete when it is not.

A thumbnail grid has exactly two options for such a section, and both are worse than text: drop it (destroying the map, which is the architecture's entire purpose) or render a placeholder box (which reads as a broken image). On sebelum-nikah, two of five clusters are empty and the whole pillar holds three articles. A grid there would be three photographs and two grey rectangles.

A number in an earlier draft of this document was wrong, and how it was caught

This section first claimed eight empty clusters across four pillars. The real figure is four across three. The error: I counted the plain-text phrase “akan datang tidak lama lagi” with a grep over the served HTML, and a Next.js document contains the page twice — once as rendered HTML and again as the serialised RSC flight payload in a <script>. Every promise line was counted twice.

Caught by the design-systems-engineer’s independent count during the build, then settled by reading document.querySelectorAll in a real browser instead of grepping text. The rest of the census survives, and the reason is specific rather than lucky: <img, id="cluster- and href="…" are anchored to unescaped attribute syntax, which the flight payload writes as \". Verified against the DOM — grep and querySelectorAll agree exactly on all three. Only the unanchored plain-text pattern was vulnerable.

3.8 UI-03's rules landed after this decision, and they make the case stronger, not weaker

UI-03 shipped docs/design/hero-image-rules.md on 31 Ogos 2026, after the decision above was taken. Its rules are binding. They do not reverse this decision; they close the imagery route considerably harder than my own argument did. Re-measured against them rather than assumed.

R2 forbids the asset the current thumbnails use. low, high and original are never eligible for a shaped slot, because all three preserve the source aspect. A pillar thumbnail box is a shaped slot. So the only legal source is a named landscape crop.

R1 makes the mobile thumbnail box unfillable. The pipeline defines exactly four crop targets, and none is square. Against the 80×80 mobile box (aspect 1.000) that UI-05 would have needed:

Crop targetAspectOutputDeviation from a 1:1 boxR1 (≤15%)
crop-4x5-mobile-cover0.8001920×240025%FAIL
crop-4x3-article-card1.3331600×120033%FAIL
crop-16x9-og1.9051200×63090%FAIL
crop-4.3x1-desktop-hero3.5202464×700252%FAIL

Under R1 there is no legal source for a square thumbnail at all, and creating one is forbidden: adding a CROP_TARGETS entry changes GEOMETRY_VERSION and re-queues every live cover through Rekognition and R2, which is an unauthorised AWS cost and therefore an owner decision. So the honest statement is not "expensive" — it is that the box does not exist and I may not create it.

Reshaping the box to a legal aspect does not rescue it either. A 16:9 thumbnail would sit at 0% deviation against crop-16x9-og, which is the smallest named landscape crop. I measured that crop across all 38 hantaran covers by HTTP:

Two seats found the same pipeline hole from opposite ends

§3.3 above reached this independently from the thumbnail side. UI-03 §5 reached it from the hero side and states it more sharply: "The pipeline generates two families of derivative, and neither one can serve a hero. There is no aspect-correct, quality-reduced derivative anywhere in it." Quality-graded assets follow the source aspect; aspect-correct assets exist at exactly one quality — full. The empty cell is the whole problem, and it is now recorded twice, from two directions, by two seats that did not coordinate.

Do NOT read "UI-03 landed" as "UI-05's reversal condition 2 is met"

UI-03 §5 requests a quality-reduced landscape crop — a q30–q50 crop-16x9-og, estimated at 80–120 KB. Across 38 covers that is 3.0–4.6 MB, still worse than the 2.02 MB this document already rejected. UI-03's pipeline request and UI-05's reversal condition 2 are different asks for different slots. UI-03 needs a 1200×630 asset made lighter; a pillar thumbnail needs an asset roughly 160–360px wide. Granting UI-03's request would not make a 38-thumbnail pillar grid viable. §8 stands unchanged.

P7 · MISSING — and it can stick

The 3-second deadline is missed

getPillarView is wrapped in withDeadline(…, 3_000). On a miss the catch block logs and leaves view at its initialiser — { clusters: [], unclustered: [], totalArticles: 0 } — and the page then renders successfully, with nothing in it.

The route declares export const revalidate = false. Because the render does not throw, Next has a valid page to cache, and it caches it indefinitely. A single DB blip during the render that populates the ISR entry pins an empty pillar until revalidateTag('articles') or revalidateTag('inspire-categories') fires from an admin write. There is no time-based revalidation to heal it.

Scope
Out of scope for UI-05, raised as its own item. This is a caching and reliability defect, not a design one, and it deserves its own sizing. Shipping P6's empty state does mitigate it — a blank page becomes a page that says something — but the mitigation is not the fix.
The fix, for whoever takes it
Do not cache a soft-failed render. Either re-throw after logging so Next does not persist the entry, or set a short revalidate window on the degraded path.

6What ships in UI-05

Two changes to src/components/inspire/pillar-body.tsx and one to src/design-system/components.css. Both exist for the same reason: if the answer to "imagery or not" is typography carries these pages, the typography has to actually hold the page.

6.1 The empty-cluster row — see P2 → P3

Specified in full in P3. Structural rule top and bottom, one row height, colour unchanged. The copy is not touched, and that is a decision rather than an oversight — see the note.

An open question from DES-07 that nobody closed, found while specifying this row

The eight live empty clusters read “Artikel untuk … akan datang tidak lama lagi.” DES-03 §7.2 C records that the approved copy for an empty surface is “Kategori ini masih kosong.” plus a way out, and says in its own words that this “already replaced production’s undatable ‘akan datang tidak lama lagi’ line”. It did not. The line is still live after DES-08.

DES-07 escalated exactly this and routed it away from design: “whether the shipped ‘akan datang tidak lama lagi’ lines should be corrected now rather than waiting for DES-08, since one of them is currently untrue on a category with 14 published articles.” That question was never answered.

I am not answering it here either, and the reason is not deference. §7.2 C’s approved copy is for a fully empty category — state P6 — and an empty cluster inside a populated pillar is a different state that the copy does not cover. Substituting it would be wrong, not just out of turn. Raised as a follow-up owned by the managing-editor, with the state distinction stated so whoever takes it is not solving the wrong one.

6.2 The article title on a pillar page is not styled at all

Found during the creative director's review; verified independently before it was believed

PillarBody puts className="t" on every article link. The only .t rules in the entire codebase are .s-row .t — descendant selectors at components.css:233 and :249 — and pillar-body.tsx contains no s-row anywhere. On pillar pages .t matches nothing and the links fall through to inherited body sans.

Same class name, two typographic identities, decided by which page you found the article on. "Hantaran Tunang Lelaki & Perempuan – Barang Apa Patut Ada Dalam Dulang?" is serif on the grid page and sans on the pillar page.

Why this matters to the decision, and not just to the codebase

Part of why the pillar list reads as an undifferentiated wall is that it was never rendering its own specification. The photography-versus-text comparison in §3 was made against text that had lost its weight, size and typeface. The image arguments stand on their own — but the comparison was unfair to the text, and that is recorded here rather than quietly benefiting the conclusion.

The fix: pillar links get their own class, .s-pillar-link, with stated values rather than a borrowed descendant selector.

The rule, in the creative director's corrected wording

Their first statement of it — "one article title must have one identity across the site" — implied that the pillar link should also match .s-row .t's weight of 600. They withdrew that phrasing as sloppy. The rule they actually hold, and the one to record: TYPEFACE is identity and never varies by context; SIZE and WEIGHT are hierarchy and vary with what else is in the row. The bug found here was serif to sans, which is an identity change. 400 against 600 is not.

The evidence that settles it. There are five className="t" call sites in the codebase: the two in pillar-body.tsx, and three others — artikel/[category]/page.tsx:790, artikel/[category]/[slug]/page.tsx:1214, and page.tsx:297. Every one of those three sits directly beside a 176px <img> inside the .s-row thumbnail grid. So 15px/600 is not the list-row identity site-wide — it is the with-a-photograph setting, three call sites for three. It exists so the text holds its own against a picture at the same optical level, and because Bodoni Moda's stems need the extra weight to survive at 15px. The pillar is the only .t call site in the codebase with no image in its row, so matching it would import a setting along with none of its reasons.

17px, and why not 15px. On a pillar the title is the entire basis for the click — no thumbnail, no excerpt, no deck — and these are long descriptive Malay titles where the discriminating fact sits after the colon ("Hantaran tunang untuk lelaki: apa yang dibalas dan harganya"). The reader is parsing a phrase, not scanning a label. 17px is the site's reading size and says read this; 15px says scan this.

400, and why not 600. On a pillar the cluster h2 (.s-h2, serif 600) sits directly above its links with a hairline between, repeating down the page. Weight separation between heading and entry is doing structural work: it is what makes the page read as a map with sections rather than one undifferentiated stack. At 600 the h2 would have to carry that on size alone. On a grid category page the h2 is nowhere near the rows, so the constraint does not exist there. And 38 consecutive semibold didone lines on the hantaran pillar is a wall; 400 gives the stack air.

Recorded explicitly as deliberate, so the next reader does not mistake it for the same class of bug: a pillar map row is lighter and larger than a grid list row, because it carries no photograph, because it is the whole click decision, and because it must stay visually subordinate to a cluster heading 20px above it. The three other .t call sites are correct as they stand — this was a pillar-only fix.

.s-row .t's -0.018em tracking is compensation for heavier stems and does not transfer; .s-pillar-link takes -0.012em, matching .hk-card-title, the system's already-tuned 400-weight serif setting.

The premise this type spec rests on, stated so it is checkable

The 17px/400 argument rests on the pillar row containing no photograph. That premise is only true because of the decision in §2. If imagery is ever added to pillar rows, this type spec must be re-derived, not carried over — the row would then be the with-a-photograph case, and 15px/600 would become the correct setting. The order matters and it was followed here: imagery was decided first, and the type was set against a row whose contents were already settled.

A pillar with no clusters and no articles currently renders an empty <div>. P6 specifies the block that replaces it. This is a missing state, not a restyle, and it is in scope because a state set with a hole in it is not delivered.

8What reverses this decision

The rationale is conditional. It is recorded this way deliberately: a blanket "topic hubs never carry images" is not defensible, and a future reader who finds one here would be right to ignore it.

Ship imagery on pillar hubs when all three are true
  1. A curated or commissioned cover set that shares one register across a whole pillar — one light, one distance to subject, one palette — with no duplicate photographs and no page illustrated by a building it does not depict.
  2. A derivative sized AND shaped for the slot exists in the pipeline. Roughly 160–360px wide, at a quality tier below full, and at an aspect within 15% of the box (UI-03 R1) — not a 1200px asset serving an 80px box, and not low, high or original, which UI-03 R2 makes ineligible for any shaped slot. This is not the same ask as UI-03 §5's, which is a quality-reduced 1200×630 crop for a hero; see §3.8.
  3. The empty-cluster problem is solved for photography, or the pillar in question has no empty clusters. A grid still cannot render a promise.

Three things that do not reverse it. More time, which changes no number here. UI-03 landing — it has now landed, and §3.8 shows it closes the imagery route harder rather than opening it. And granting UI-03 §5's pipeline request, which is a different derivative for a different slot. None of them fixes the office block, and condition 1 is the one that was decisive.

How to re-run every measurement in this document

  1. The 44-URL census. Fetch each /artikel/{slug} and count <img, class="s-row", class="s-card", id="cluster-. Pillars are the rows with cluster- and zero s-row.
  2. The byte figures. Page transfer with curl -H 'Accept-Encoding: br'; asset bytes individually against images.hellokahwin.com. The rig at scripts/measure-page.mjs cannot measure image weight — cross-origin resources report 0 bytes without Timing-Allow-Origin.
  3. Intrinsic dimensions — never img.naturalWidth on a page element. On an element carrying a srcset with w descriptors it returns intrinsic width divided by the density derived from sizes, so it is wrong even when the image is fully loaded. Read from a detached Image() on currentSrc. Verified here: sizes="176px" on a 1200px asset reports 176, because 1200 ÷ (1200 ÷ 176) = 176.
  4. Never count a plain-text phrase with a grep over the served HTML. A Next.js document carries the page twice — rendered HTML plus the RSC flight payload — so an unanchored text pattern returns exactly double. Count in the DOM with querySelectorAll, or anchor the pattern to unescaped attribute syntax (<img, id="cluster-), which the flight payload escapes and therefore cannot match.
  5. Computed typography. getComputedStyle at 390 and 1280 on the live pages, not on local CSS.
  6. Contrast. Sample rendered pixels off a canvas. Every token is oklch(); Chrome returns lab() and an rgb parser reports confident garbage.
