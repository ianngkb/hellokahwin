Carian & Katalog

The site has a search box. Measured against production on 28 Ogos 2026, it returns zero results for 84.3% of the queries readers actually arrive on, covering 70.9% of a month's impressions, including the single highest-clicking query the site has. This is the flow specification that fixes that, and the catalogue decisions that go with it. Every screen is drawn at 360 px against real Malay content taken from the live site.

One of those six is false

The site has six live articles about dewan, two of them on that same page under a different heading. A reader who searches "dewan" is served; a reader who browses to the section named for it is told to come back later. This is the clearest single argument for why search and browse have to be designed together.

/artikel is not a catalogue

The page links to 12 of the 86 articles: one lead, two secondary, a "Terkini" grid of nine, and a category list. All twelve are from Hantaran & Mas Kahwin, because they are the most recent. The other 74 articles are reachable only through category pages, six of which are not in the navigation.

Speed is a design constraint here, not a footnote

First-request timings for the fifteen category pages, curl, 28 Ogos 2026: twelve returned in 0.37–1.83 s; /artikel/moden-kontemporari took 12.5 s, /artikel/fotografi-videografi 22.5 s, /artikel/minimalis-mewah 23.5 s. RISK-08 owns the cause. The consequence for this specification is that a search which only works after JavaScript has loaded and hydrated is a search that sometimes does not exist.

3The decisions, and what was rejected

3.1 Search opens in place. It never navigates.

A panel over the current page, opened from the header on every page type, dismissed with Escape, returning focus to the trigger. The URL does not change until the reader commits.

Rejected — the current jump to /artikel#cari. It throws away the article the reader was in, and on a cold category render measured at 12–23 s today, that is an expensive thing to throw away. It also does not do what it appears to: the autofocus effect runs once on mount, so a reader already on /artikel who taps the header search icon changes the hash and gets no focus at all.

Rejected — a permanently visible field in the header. The mobile header is 56 px and already carries a wordmark plus a horizontally scrolling category rail below it. A 44 px field would take a third of the viewport's vertical furniture to serve an action most readers take once.

3.2 There is a results page at a real URL: /cari?q=…

Four reasons, in order of weight:

  1. The dropdown cannot hold the state that matters. Under the proposed rule, 76.7% of answered demand resolves to one or two articles, and 23.3% resolves to nothing. A five-row dropdown is adequate for the first and useless for the second. The zero-result case needs a page.
  2. The back button. The site's core reading task is comparative: a reader checking mas kahwin across states moves list → article → back, repeatedly. A URL makes that the browser's job.
  3. It works without JavaScript. A plain <form action="/cari" method="get"> submits and renders on the server. Given the cold-render measurements, the no-JS path is a real path.
  4. It is countable. A query string is loggable; a client-side dropdown is not.
SEO, needs head-of-seo-content sign-off

/cari emits <meta name="robots" content="noindex,follow"> and is disallowed in robots.txt. Internal search results are the textbook thin-page generator; follow keeps link equity flowing to the articles they point at. /cari is not added to the sitemap. No ItemList schema is emitted on it. Written here as a proposal for DES-09 to ratify, not a decision taken on that seat's behalf.

3.3 The matching rule, stated so it can be tested

  1. Normalise both query and index: lowercase, strip punctuation, fold kawin → kahwin, maskahwin → mas kahwin, maskawin → mas kahwin. Keep digits: 6.7% of queries carry a year and the titles carry it too.
  2. Stage 1. All query tokens present in title or slug. This is the primary index and it is the reason "mas kahwin" returns nine articles rather than the whole hantaran category.
  3. Stage 2, only if stage 1 is empty. Widen to excerpt, category name and section heading.
  4. Stage 3, only if stage 2 is empty. Rank by proportion of tokens matched, keep anything at 60% or above, and label the block as approximate. An approximate match presented as an answer is worse than nothing on a site people use to decide what to pay a family.
  5. Rank exact phrase in title, then token count matched, then shorter title. Stop-words drop from the AND set but still count toward ranking.

The response gains a total alongside the array, and honours limit and offset. Without a total, "12 hasil" cannot be written truthfully, and every count in the screens below depends on it.

3.4 Pagination or infinite scroll: neither, then pagination

Decision

No paging mechanism below 60 items in a set. Above 60, real paginated ?page=n URLs with anchor links. Infinite scroll is rejected outright and should not be revisited without new evidence.

The largest set on the site is 38, and it already renders in full on one page grouped into five named sections. Pagination today would add a click and a crawl hop to solve a problem that does not exist. The decision worth making now is the threshold and the mechanism, so the question is settled before Hantaran & Mas Kahwin crosses it.

AxisInfinite scrollPaginated ?page=n
Crawlability Scroll-triggered fetch leaves items 25+ with no crawlable link. Googlebot does not scroll. Making it crawlable means building paginated URLs underneath it anyway. Every item sits behind an <a href>. Crawl depth is arithmetic and predictable.
Back button Back returns to a remounted list at scroll 0 unless the implementation restores both loaded pages and offset. That restoration breaks on soft navigation and on bfcache misses, silently. The browser's own history entry. Nothing to implement, nothing to break.
Return to position Not addressable. There is no URL for "where I was". ?page=2 is a position that can be bookmarked or sent to somebody.
Fit to this site Solves a problem at 500 items. The site has 86. Costs nothing until the threshold trips.

On the link-equity argument. SEO-02 removed 79 rel=nofollow links and added 68 real ones. Introducing a pattern whose default failure mode is hiding links from crawlers moves directly against that work. That is the reasoning; the ruling belongs to head-of-seo-content, and this section is written to be signed or overruled.

"Load more" as a middle path. Acceptable only as progressive enhancement layered on top of a real <a href="?page=2">: the anchor ships in the HTML, script upgrades the click to an in-place append and pushes the new URL. Crawlers and no-JS readers follow the anchor. If the button is a <button> with no href, it is infinite scroll with a tap instead of a scroll and is rejected on the same three grounds.

3.5 Filters: one, and it is content rather than chrome

No filter panel. Eleven of fifteen categories hold four articles or fewer. A facet control over a three-item set is furniture pretending to be a feature.

Two mechanisms instead:

  • Section jump links on any category with three or more sections — today Hantaran (5), Sebelum Nikah (5), Pelamin (4), Nikah (4), Ucapan (4). The editorial sub-sections already exist as h2s; this makes them navigable. Filtering by the taxonomy the editors actually built.
  • A negeri table on the mas kahwin cluster, as an article component rather than a control: all sixteen states, each linking to the article that covers it, and the states with no article saying so plainly. This is the structural fix for the mas kahwin terengganu failure and it turns a quarter of site demand into one navigable object.

3.6 Sort: none, and here is the trigger that changes that

Pillar categories are ordered editorially, not chronologically. A "Terbaru" control would let a reader destroy the only curation on the page, in exchange for reordering eight items. The Real Wedding Edit categories are chronological but hold one to six items each, where sorting is meaningless.

Sort ships when a single set exceeds 20 items and carries no editorial order. Today nothing meets both conditions. Stated so the omission reads as a decision rather than an oversight.

3.7 How search relates to browsing

  1. Search never replaces browse. The results page always ends with the full category index, counts included.
  2. Every result carries its category as a link. A search is a doorway onto a shelf, not a vending machine.
  3. An empty section offers what does exist instead of promising a date. The Dewan & venue majlis case above is the test: six relevant articles exist, so the section links to them.
  4. The zero-result page hands the reader back to browse with the four largest categories and their real counts.

Structure

  • One role="combobox" on the input. The listbox is a single ul role="listbox". The shipped markup nests a listbox inside a listbox; that is invalid and gets flattened.
  • A visible <label> reading Cari, not a placeholder standing in for one. A placeholder disappears the moment a reader types, taking the field's accessible name with it.
  • The aria-live="polite" region is present in the DOM from first render and starts empty. The shipped one is created at the same moment its content arrives, which is the classic reason an announcement is never made.
  • Announcements, once per settled query: 9 hasil. · 1 hasil. · Tiada hasil. · Carian gagal. Nothing fires during typing or loading.

Focus visibility

Measured against the live stylesheet, 28 Ogos 2026

The field's focus ring ships as focus:ring-ring/30, which composites to #b7b6b4 on --background #fcfbfa: 1.96:1. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for a focus indicator. At 50% it reaches 3.44:1 and at 60% it reaches 4.78:1. Specified: a 2 px ring in --ring at full opacity with a 2 px offset, which measures 17.81:1 and matches the ink the rest of the page is set in.

Targets and motion

  • Every interactive element is at least 44×44 px. Result rows land at 56 px including padding, chips at 38 px height inside a 44 px hit area.
  • Panel entry is a 120 ms fade, no slide. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce it appears with no transition. Skeletons never shimmer.
  • The panel does not move the page behind it. Background scroll is locked while it is open, and the scroll position is restored on close.

9What this does not cover

Stated so the build does not have to guess whether an omission was deliberate.

  • Any implementation. DES-06 is design only. The build is a separate item and has not been sized.
  • The visual language. Screens are drawn in the tokens production ships today so the layout can be judged against something real. Typeface, palette and scale belong to DES-01/02/03, and every screen here should be re-rendered once that direction is chosen. Nothing in this document depends on the palette staying as it is.
  • Dark mode. The public surface ships light only; .hk-public defines one palette. Whether it gains a dark one is DES-03's call, not this item's.
  • Venue search. The type=venues parameter is accepted and ignored by the live API. Venue entity pages are SEO-04. Search cannot return what does not exist, and the placeholder copy here deliberately does not promise it.
  • Category names, groupings, and whether the six off-nav categories should merge. Editorial, and above this item.
  • Whether an empty section should render at all. Raised in C3; owned by managing-editor.
  • The article reading experience — measure, scroll depth, in-article navigation. Adjacent and not in this item's definition of done.
  • Search on the admin console. Different surface, different reader, different problem.
  • Autocomplete of the query itself, as opposed to results. It needs a term dictionary the site does not have, and the four fixed suggestions in E2 cover the same ground until the query log exists.

Handover

  • design-systems-engineer — components needed: the search panel (Radix Dialog, focus trap and inert come free), the combobox listbox, the result row, the count chip, the section jump rail, the skeleton row, the pagination pair. All consume semantic tokens only.
  • head-of-seo-content — three things need your signature, all in this document: /cari as noindex,follow and robots-disallowed (§3.2); pagination over infinite scroll and the 60-item threshold (§3.4); and the internal-link change from C1, which adds one inbound link to all 86 articles and should be checked against the SEO-02 model before it is built.
  • Whoever builds this — the API needs three changes before any screen above is buildable: a total in the response, an honoured limit and offset, and an error status that is distinguishable from an empty array. Without the third, F1 cannot exist and the defect that costs the most stays shipped.
