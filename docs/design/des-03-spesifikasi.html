03

Colour — two palettes, one exposed, every ratio measured

The dark-mode ruling, and exactly what it obliges

The decision of 2026-07-14 stands: dark is not reachable by a reader, there is no ThemeProvider and there is no toggle. That is the CEO's ruling of 28 Ogos 2026 and this document does not reopen it. What the ruling does not do is excuse the system from defining dark. So: light is complete on the base, dark redefines only the semantic layer, and no colour anywhere has its sole definition inside a dark block. Both palettes are specified; one is exposed. That is what globals.css already asks for in its own comment — keep this palette in sync so dark can be switched on later — and it is the only arrangement in which that comment can be true.

3.1  Two layers, and the rule that makes the ruling enforceable

Every colour value in the system lives in the primitive layer, on the bare :root, light and dark alike. Nothing consumes a primitive directly. Components read only the semantic layer, and the dark block redefines semantics by pointing them at primitives that are already defined. Switching dark on later is then one class on <html> and nothing else — no new hex, no second stylesheet, no audit.

Why the alphas differ between the themes

A tint of ink on parchment and a tint of parchment on night are not symmetric — light ink on a dark ground reads stronger at the same alpha. The two boundary values were tuned to the same target, the 3:1 non-text floor, and land at 3.06:1 and 3.05:1. The alphas are numbers in the primitive layer, not colours, so the dark block still introduces no colour of its own.

The check the DSE runs before merging

Grep the stylesheet for a hex literal or an rgb() inside any .hk-dark, [data-theme] or prefers-color-scheme block. The correct count is zero. One hit means a colour exists that light has never seen, and the palettes have started to drift — which is the exact failure the 2026-07-14 comment was written to prevent.

3.4  The one-colour rule, and how the count actually holds at four

DES-01 fixed the ceiling: any single page renders in at most four palette values plus photography. That is the one thing every premium reference in the market survey agreed on — Carats & Cake 2, Dentelle 5, Alia Bastamam 5, PartySlate 6, against nikahsatu's 50 and mingguanwanita's 66. It is also the cheapest discipline available: it costs nothing and it is the thing a reader feels as someone is in charge here.

Obeying it literally used to be impossible, because DES-01 also listed a raised ground and a hairline as separate tokens, which takes a page with rules and stripes to six. The resolution: every rule, stripe, separator and boundary is an alpha of the ink, so it is a tint of a colour already on the page rather than a new one. A page therefore renders in exactly four hex values.

The decision that keeps the third row at four, and it is mine

Oxblood and gold never appear on the same page. On a surface that carries an alert, oxblood takes the accent slot and gold stands down — labels and credits render in --fg-muted instead. This is not a contrivance to save the count. An error screen with a decorative gold eyebrow above a red warning is a page with two accents competing for the same attention, and the warning loses. One accent per surface; the surface decides which.

3.5  Three colour rules that are not negotiable downstream

No text over a photograph

Anywhere. A contrast ratio against a photograph cannot be measured, so it cannot be asserted, so the pairing does not exist. Headlines, decks and credits all sit on the ground. Every credit in every screen below does.

Thread gold draws nothing that matters

2.95:1 on parchment. It fails the 3:1 non-text floor, so it may not border an input, ring a focus state, or carry text at any size. It is a decorative rule. If a boundary needs to be seen, it is --boundary.

No pure black, no pure white

Measured: 18.52:1 against 15.39:1, for no readability gain and a harsher page. Every measured premium brand in the market survey softens at least one side. This softens both, which is a stronger reading of the same instinct.

One. Vertical space between sibling blocks is set by the parent's gap, never by a margin on the child. A child that carries its own margin collapses differently depending on what precedes it, which is how a rhythm becomes a set of exceptions.

328 px is a measurement, not a preference

DES-07 ran all 86 live titles through the real card box at three widths. At the shipped 156 px two-column card, 57 of 86 are cut and the worst loses 46 of 95 characters. At 236 px, 5 are cut. The rail is not allowed to be empty

DES-02 rendered direction A and found 300 px of dead rail beside the photograph — a hole for a screen and a half. How to read the frames

Phone frames are exactly 360 px — the narrowest supported viewport, and the one DES-07 measured every failure at. Desktop frames are exactly 1200 px. Both scroll inside their own rail rather than resizing the page, so a measurement taken off this document is the measurement specified. Light and dark are drawn side by side and do not follow your theme: the site ships light only, and this document has to show both regardless. 7.5  H6 — the homepage diversity rule, written so it can fire

What this corrects, and why it is a defect in this document rather than in the build

§5.3 asserted a diversity rule and sent the reader to “H6 in §7”. No rule H6 existed. Every word-boundary H6 match in this artifact was three base64 fragments inside embedded WebP data plus the cross-reference itself, and §7 carried the word diversity zero times and categor zero times. The rule was asserted, drawn, and never written, so nobody could have built it and nobody could have failed to build it. Sprint 04's finding applies exactly: the parts of this document written as enforceable constraints shipped, and the parts written as prose and a drawing did not ship at all — same document, same author, same sprint. H6 is therefore written below as a constraint with an extraction rule, a tie-break, a fallback and a checker script, not as a sentence about variety.

IdWhat it denotesWhere it is defined

H6 — homepage category diversity. Normative.

H6.0 — the set, and how it is extracted. The homepage item set is every article link the homepage renders, in DOM order, deduplicated by path with the first occurrence winning. An article link is an href matching /artikel/<kategori>/<slug> — exactly two path segments after /artikel/. A one-segment link is a category link and is never an item, which is what keeps the masthead, the breadcrumb and the footer out of the count. An item's category is <kategori>. Nothing else is consulted: no data attribute, no CMS field, no heading text. s[1] is the hero, N is the number of items, and cap = ceil(N/3).

H6.1 — share cap. No category supplies more than cap items. At N = 13 the cap is 5.

H6.2 — run cap. No two consecutive items share a category. Maximum run length is 1.

H6.3 — distinct floor. The set names at least F = min(4, K, N − cap + 1) distinct categories, where K is the number of categories holding at least one published article. The third term is not decoration: without it H6.3 contradicts H6.1 at small N, because a floor above N − cap + 1 forbids any category from reaching the cap H6.1 explicitly permits. At N = 4 the floor is 3, not 4. That contradiction was in the first draft of this clause and was caught by the pass-short.html fixture, not by reading it.

H6.4 — the tie-break, because recency alone does not order this corpus. Slots are filled 1 to N. Slot 1 takes the highest-ranked article whose cover is class O or class P (§5.3, §6.3); no H6 clause can bind at slot 1, so hero eligibility and H6 cannot conflict there. Every later slot takes the highest-ranked remaining article that keeps H6.1 and H6.2 true. Rank, applied in order until one comparison decides: (1) published date, most recent first; (2) then the number already placed from that article's category, fewest first; (3) then category slug, ascending byte order; (4) then article slug, ascending byte order. Every comparison is total, so one corpus always yields one set. This clause is load-bearing: on the sitemap of 01 September 2026, 24 articles carry one identical timestamp and 19 carry another — rank 1 alone leaves 24 items unordered and the homepage non-deterministic. Rejected: ranking diversity first, that is (2) before (1). It produces a well-mixed homepage that buries what was published yesterday, and §5.3 gives the homepage the job of being the record of what is new. Diversity is a constraint here, not a preference.

H6.5 — the fallback, when the corpus cannot satisfy it. H6.1 is satisfiable at a given N if and only if Σx min(published(x), cap) ≥ N. H6.2 is then satisfiable automatically, because cap = ceil(N/3) never exceeds ceil(N/2), the multiplicity above which no arrangement can avoid adjacency. One test therefore decides the whole question. If it fails, relax in this fixed order, one step at a time, never further than the slot requires: (1) relax H6.2 for that slot only — a run of 2 is permitted, a run of 3 never is; (2) then relax H6.1 for that slot only, cap + 1, and return to step 1 for the following slot before spending another +1; (3) then truncate — render a shorter set. The homepage is valid at any N ≥ 4. Never repeat an article and never pad to reach a target length. (4) Below N = 4 the homepage runs H3, the no-hero variant; with zero published articles it renders .s-empty (§8), not the blank page §7.4 records shipping. The order is deliberate: an adjacent pair is monotony, one category owning the page is the failure §5.3 measured. Monotony is the cheaper thing to spend, so it is spent first.

H6.6 — DOM order is the order. H6 is measured on DOM order, which is also tab order and the order a screen reader announces. Homepage items may not be visually reordered away from source order — no order, no grid-auto-flow: dense, no *-reverse on the item container. A page that satisfies the checker while reading in a different sequence to a sighted reader has satisfied nothing.

H6 needs no build input this document does not already have. §12 names “category counts available at render time” as an unresolved build dependency. H6 does not touch it: an item's category is in its own URL, so the rule is evaluable on the rendered page today, with no CMS change and nothing added to the markup.

The strongest argument against this, answered

The objection, stated as strongly as it can be

A record-first layout with a library that only trusts four photographs at scale will read as a government gazette, not a wedding publication. Direction B put a full-bleed photograph on every card because a wedding site's job is to make a reader feel something before it informs them. Warkah leads with a rate table. That is the argument, and it does not go away because the owner already chose A.

Three things are true at once, and none of them is a matter of taste.

The intent ground is a fact about this site, not a preference

The highest-impression page on hellokahwin.com is a rate table. A reader arriving from mas kahwin Perak is not browsing for inspiration; DES-06 measured 84.3% of arrivals hunting a named thing. A layout optimised for the feeling of scrolling a gallery serves the reader B was built for, and that is not the reader who is actually here in the numbers we have.

The inventory ground is not a design failure to route around

Four of eleven library photographs survive enlargement. A layout that needs eleven of eleven to look considered is a photography commission wearing a layout costume — DES-02's finding, restated in §6. Warkah's five-device identity in §1.2 is deliberately checkable with the photograph covered, which is the property that lets it ship against the library that exists rather than the one a full-bleed design would require.

Where the objection is right, and what this document does about it

It is right that a page of nothing but fields and rules can go cold. That is why the credit line is designed rather than appended (§1.2's fifth device), why every photograph that does appear runs at its real crop rather than a stock thumbnail, and why §6 spends real space defending which photographs earn a large placement rather than papering over the gap with a placeholder. The warmth is supposed to come from specificity — the real rate, the real state, the real date it was checked — not from a photograph doing the work the text should be doing. Whether that reads as warm rather than clinical is exactly the kind of judgement a canvas review answers and a written rebuttal cannot; DES-02's rendered comps are the evidence for that half of the argument, and this document does not re-litigate what the owner already decided from them.
