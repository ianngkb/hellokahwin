Three directions, one register. Pick one.

DES-02, 28 Ogos 2026. The article is the live mas kahwin ikut negeri page, drawn three ways at 1440px, 390px, light and dark, plus the same three applied to a real category of 38 articles and to four cases that break a static mock.

What you are choosing, and what you are not

This is the one gate. After you name a direction, the type scale, colour ramps, spacing, photographic selects, component styling and page composition are mine and do not come back to you.

What is already settled and not on the table here: the register (a publication of record, warmly printed), the locked palette, one webfont rather than four, the serif confined to the masthead and the h1, no motif applied as ornament, no text cards. All three directions obey all of it. They differ in composition and in how much of the page a photograph is allowed to own.

The three

A · Warkah — the record

The answer sits above the fold and the photograph works inside the grid. The five facts a reader came for — lowest rate, highest rate, how many jurisdictions set nothing, when it was last checked — are a ruled panel beside the headline. The photograph is a column-width figure below it, credited on the same line as its caption.

The case: our highest-impression page is a table of rates. Six of fourteen jurisdictions fix no minimum, and no competitor prints that. Putting the record first is the direction that makes our one real advantage visible in the first screen. It is also the cheapest to load, and 64% of impressions arrive on mobile.

The cost: it is the least emotional of the three. A couple planning a wedding meets a gazette. If the answer is felt as cold, this is the direction that earns that.

B · Dulang — the object

The photograph is the page. Full-bleed cover, 4:5 on a phone, with the headline on its own parchment ground overlapping the picture rather than sitting on it. One large photograph per section below. The record is a quiet run of fields inside the measure.

The case: every competitor measured for DES-01 is photographically empty — a complete kursus kahwin guide on kahwinstudio ships two images. Restraint everywhere else exists so that one picture can land, and this is the direction that actually spends it. Our best frames are close-up objects, and they deserve the width: a brass tepak sirih on a dulang, a Quran in a white cage, stacked dulang under red cloth and lace.

The cost: two of them. It spends the LCP budget on the first screen for a reader who arrived wanting a number. And it fails when a cover is missing — see test 2. That failure needs a written rule, not a hope.

C · Margin — the annotated reference

The page is a spread rather than a stack. A 68ch text column runs beside a permanent 268px rail that carries what the record needs: date checked, jurisdiction count, range, sources, the section index, and the photograph at margin scale. The table is the one moment the spread opens across both.

The case: it is the only direction that gives the meta a permanent home instead of a panel that appears once and never returns. It is how a reference book has always carried its marginalia, and it still reads as considered. It also handles a long Malay title best of the three, because the text column is wide.

The cost: the rail is a desktop idea, and 64% of impressions are mobile. It is drawn lying down as a ruled band above the body, which is a real answer rather than a fallback — but the thing that makes this direction distinctive is the thing most of our readers never see.

What I recommend, and the best argument against it

A · Warkah. The reader arrives with a question that has a correct answer, and the register the CEO approved is built on that. A puts the answer where the question is. It also protects the mobile budget, which is where most of the audience is.

The strongest objection: you have argued for months that competitors are photographically empty, and then chosen the direction that makes the photograph smallest. B is the one that actually differentiates. That is fair. A does not shrink the photograph, it constrains it to the column and puts it below the record, at roughly 40% of the first screen rather than 100%. And the library decides this more than my taste does. I pulled eleven photographs off our own CDN for this canvas, from the pages that use them. Four are close-up object frames and hold up at any size. Seven are documentary group snapshots, several of them 2009-era Flickr, and they fall apart above about 300px. Look at the B catalogue on page 3: three of its six covers are group snapshots at 330px, and you can see the difference. A direction that stakes the whole first screen on the picture is betting on the smaller half of our library. If the answer is B, a picture commission comes first, and it is not budgeted.

Colour, and the rule that keeps it

Four values on any page, plus photography. The one thing every reference in DES-01 agreed on was colour count, and it costs nothing to obey. A tint of one of the four is not a fifth colour; a fifth hex is. Every rule, stripe and boundary below is drawn as a tint of the ink or the parchment, which is why these pages hold at four.

No text sits on a photograph anywhere in these three. A ratio against a picture cannot be measured, so it does not get asserted. Where B puts the headline over the cover it is on a parchment card, and every credit sits on the ground.

Breakpoints

360px, 390px, 768px, 1024px, 1440px. The desktop column is 1200px with 40px gutters in A and B, 1240px in C to carry the rail. The body measure is capped at 68ch at every width, which is the single largest readability change available: the live article currently sets 14px in a container up to 1,248px wide.

What this canvas does not settle

  • Dark stays unreachable to a reader. The 2026-07-14 decision holds. It is drawn here because it has to be designed alongside light, not translated from it afterwards.
  • The photographs are the site's own licensed library, at the pairings the live pages already use. I did not reshuffle them to flatter any direction; that is why B's catalogue carries three weak covers.
  • The stress artboard's pictures are stand-ins. It tests wrapping and emptiness, not cover selection, and says so on the artboard.
  • Search, filters, loading and error states belong to DES-06 and DES-07 and are not redrawn.
