## The finding that matters most

Malaysian Malay wedding photography barely exists under an open licence.

Wikimedia Commons returns nothing usable for `hantaran`, `dulang hantaran`,
`mas kahwin`, `kad kahwin`, `kenduri kahwin`, `bunga telur` or `inai`. Not weak
results. No results.

What it does return in volume is Indonesian: Melayu Deli (Medan), Riau, Batam,
Palembang, Mandailing. A search for `inai pengantin` returns 68 results over
1500px, and every one of the top 25 is Indonesian Melayu Deli studio work.
`bunga telur` returns one good image, shot in Medan. `hantaran` returns Javanese
and Sundanese *seserahan*.

Style guide §13.3 names this exact trap: "no Indonesian seserahan photograph
presented as Malaysian hantaran." Had I matched on filenames alone, I would have
illustrated most of P4 and P5 with Indonesian photography, which is the outcome
the brief was written to prevent, and it was one search away.

Pexels broke the deadlock rather than Commons, because of two photographers
whose Malaysian location I could verify from their profile pages. §3 covers that.

## 2. Where I declined to put an image, and why

The brief asked me to decide honestly whether a photograph helps. Of the 28
articles now in play, 22 get no photograph.

The eight live C2.4 articles get nothing. They compare mas kahwin rates state by
state. A reader arriving on `mas kahwin johor` wants a number, and no photograph
moves them toward it. Their own review board reached the same conclusion. I did
search `mas kahwin` and `mahar` in case a photograph of the handover existed.
Nothing does.

`syarat-sah-nikah`, `borang-nikah` and `lafaz-taklik` get nothing. They are
lists of legal conditions, gazetted forms with fees, and gazetted legal text,
already carried by graphics that show the actual structure. A stock photograph
beside a table of Perak form fees is decoration, and decoration on a legal page
reads as padding.

`checklist-kahwin`, `bajet-kahwin` and `pakej-dewan-kahwin` get nothing. The
first two are sequence and arithmetic. For `pakej-dewan-kahwin` I wanted one,
because the article turns on what "500 kerusi banquet dan juruteknik" looks like
against "ruang dan kunci", so I searched for a Malaysian dewan interior dressed
for a kenduri. The only open-licensed hall interiors I found were Turkish and
Indonesian. The article goes without rather than substitute.

`rukun-nikah` gets one, because the fifth rukun is the sighah, and a reader who
has never attended an akad cannot picture the moment the article turns on.

`harga-sewa-dewan-kahwin` gets one, because the article argues that the
RM60-an-hour tier is a real, plain kampung hall. Showing that hall is the
argument rather than decoration.

Three of the six new P4 and P5 articles also get nothing, and these are the
painful ones, because they are the most visual articles on the map:

- `inai-tangan-pengantin` gets nothing. There is no Malaysian berinai photograph
  under an open licence. What exists is Indian, Moroccan and Pakistani henna. On
  an article that spends a section on why inai hitam is dangerous, an Indian
  bridal henna photograph would be both culturally wrong and quietly misleading
  about what is being discussed.
- `contoh-kad-jemputan-kahwin` gets nothing. Searches for `kad kahwin` return
  Japanese and Chinese invitation cards and one 1980 Anglo-Polish invitation.
  No Malay wedding invitation exists under an open licence.
- `bunga-telur` gets nothing. The single good bunga telur photograph on Commons
  was shot in Medan. The article's whole job is distinguishing bunga telur from
  bunga pahar and bunga rampai, so an Indonesian substitute would undercut the
  distinction it is drawing.

`P7-A3-doa-majlis-pertunangan` gets nothing by argument rather than absence. The
article's case is that a pertunangan should not be staged like a wedding.
Illustrating it with a lavish engagement dais would contradict the text.

## 3. How the Indonesian trap was avoided, and what it cost

Filenames are not evidence of origin. Two examples from this run:

- `File:Imam Shah JPG.jpg`, described on Commons as "Imam di majlis perkahwinan
  Nadia Brian pada 2019", is a young man in a navy three-piece suit at a
  banquet. There is no imam in it. Rejected.
- `File:PELAMIN.jpg` is not a pelamin. It is a school cultural-corner display
  holding a gamelan set, baju melayu on stands and two ornate chairs.
  Presenting it as a pelamin would misinform. Rejected.

Every candidate in this run was downloaded and looked at before acceptance. That
is what caught both, and it should be non-negotiable in any future sourcing
brief.

For the Pexels images I also verified the photographer's stated location on
their profile page, because the subject matter alone could not settle Malaysia
against Indonesia:

- mohd hasan (`pexels.com/@mdkamal`) states Terengganu, Malaysia, with the bio
  "mostly captured in Malaysia" and 10.5K images. Four accepted.
- Khairul M Hannafi (`pexels.com/@khairulhannafi`) states Putrajaya, Malaysia,
  GIWA Photography, wedding and event photographer. One accepted.

Two Pexels images I wanted were dropped on that same test. `qodak stx` has a
good shot of seven modern hantaran trays but states no location, so I could not
establish Malaysian origin. `Solehuddin Din` has a strong studio portrait of
baju melayu with tanjak and keris, also with no stated location. The garment is
shared Malay heritage, so the image is not demonstrably wrong, but I could not
caption it as Malaysian. I have left it as an editor's-call candidate rather
than an acceptance: `pexels.com/photo/man-in-traditionaal-costume-13305543/`.

Two Commons images were dropped on quality and dignity rather than licence.
`Kahwin - panoramio` shows a genuine Malaysian nikah, but at 2011
compact-camera quality and with a room full of identifiable private guests. Its
companion shows guests mid-meal. Both licences were fine. A credit line does not
make it acceptable to put strangers eating on a commercial page.

## 4. Caveats recorded against specific assets

These are in the register and must not be lost when the images are used.

1. `S-pengantin-selepas-akad-azlan-dupree.jpg` is 1500×1000 and that is the
   ceiling. Flickr's largest public size for it is Large 1600. It works as an
   in-article image and must not be promoted to a cover, because
   `crop-4.3x1-desktop-hero` would render it soft. Kit spec §2.3 confirms only
   covers are crop-constrained, which is why the in-article use is sound.

2. Five of the 13 are CC BY-SA and carry a share-alike obligation. A crop through
   the normal pipeline is fine. Anything beyond a crop makes a derivative that
   must be released under the same licence. An engineer should know this before
   adding an overlay or a colour treatment.

3. Four images contain identifiable people and carry that note. The Azlan DuPree
   file carries a formal personality-rights notice on Commons. The licences cover
   copyright rather than the depicted person. Editorial use on a wedding page
   with a visible credit is the intended and defensible use. A promotional or
   endorsement context is not, and the Pexels Licence bars it explicitly.

4. The Flickr origin corrected the Commons description. Commons files
   `Malay couple.jpg` under bersanding, but the Flickr original is titled "Ali +
   Lin // Ijab Qabul". My caption describes what is visible, a couple after the
   akad, rather than repeating either label. Checking the origin changed the
   caption, so it earned its place in the process.

## 5. A style-guide decision I made, for the record

Style guide §13.1 sets the credit format as `Kredit: {Nama pemilik}`. A CC
licence additionally requires the licence itself to be named. I have written
these credits as:

> `Kredit: Azlan DuPree (CC BY 2.0)`

with `creditUrl` pointing at the file page. This is the smallest honest
extension of §13.1. It still names the owner exactly as §13.1 demands, and it
satisfies the attribution term we are actually bound by. I am treating this as
settled and will fold it into §13.1 as the stock-photography case. I am raising
it here so it is a recorded decision rather than a silent divergence.

CC0 and public-domain assets are credited too, though neither licence requires
it. That is the owner's rule rather than the licensor's, and it is the right
side to err on.

## 6. Register note for whoever maintains it

Register README §7.4 says the file is "sorted by `asset_id`". It is not, and it
was not before this run. It is grouped by class in insertion order: `HK-L-*`,
then `HK-G-*`, then `HK-C-*`. My 13 rows append as `HK-P-*`, sorted within
themselves, following the file's actual practice rather than its documented one.
Either the README should say "grouped by class, appended within class" or the
file should be re-sorted. It is a one-line fix either way. I have not made it
unilaterally because it touches a document somebody else owns the wording of.

The file is still UTF-8 without BOM: 713 data rows, none malformed, all 20
columns.

## 7. The gap, and what I think it is worth spending to close

The brief asked for a recommendation. Here it is, with the reasoning.

### What is genuinely missing

Six subjects have no usable licensed photograph anywhere I looked. All six are
high-intent search subjects for us:

| Subject | What exists | What we would need |
|---|---|---|
| Hantaran gubahan | Two Malaysian images, both from one Pexels contributor | A set, not two frames |
| Inai / berinai | Indian, Moroccan, Pakistani henna only | Malaysian bridal inai, hands in frame |
| Kad kahwin | Japanese, Chinese, one 1980 Anglo-Polish card | Malay invitation cards, several styles |
| Bunga telur and bunga pahar | One image, shot in Medan | Both forms, side by side, Malaysian |
| Dewan dressed for a kenduri | Turkish and Indonesian halls | A Malaysian hall, set up, wide |
| Doorgift | Nothing | A doorgift table |

### My recommendation, in order

**First, and cheapest: ask mohd hasan.** He is a Terengganu photographer with
10.5K images already released under a licence that permits commercial use, and
his work covers hantaran, bersanding and pelamin at 6000px. Two of his images
are already in our register. We are within our rights to use everything he has
posted without asking, which is exactly why asking is worth doing: a short note
saying we credit him by name on every use, and would he consider shooting inai,
bunga telur and a doorgift table, costs nothing and may return a body of work no
budget would otherwise buy. He is one name, he is findable, and the licence
question is already settled. **This is the highest return per ringgit on the
list, because the ringgit figure is zero.**

I have not contacted him. Outbound contact is the owner's decision and the brief
is explicit. He belongs at the top of the list already being prepared.

**Second: commission one day of photography, not a programme.** One
photographer, one day, one styled set covering inai, kad kahwin, bunga telur,
bunga pahar and a doorgift table. These are still-life subjects. They need
props, a table and window light rather than a wedding, a couple and model
releases. A single day buys the five hardest gaps outright, and commissioned
work enters the register as `licenseClass: O` with the rights assigned, which is
cleaner than anything we can source.

I would put this ahead of a vendor-licensing programme. A vendor programme
means chasing many people for permission to use images we did not art-direct and
cannot re-crop freely. One commissioned day means we own the result.

**Third, and only if the first two are done: the dewan interior.** It is the one
gap that genuinely needs access to a real venue rather than a table. It is also
the least urgent, because `pakej-dewan-kahwin` is a cost article that works on
tables. Fold it into a venue visit if one happens anyway.

### What I would not spend on

**Do not buy a stock subscription for this.** The problem is not licence cost,
it is that the pictures do not exist, and paying does not change that. I checked
Getty on 25 Aug 2026:

- `hantaran` returns **one** result, and it is a Ganesha statue on betel leaves
  from a South Indian Hindu ceremony. Not one Malay hantaran image on Getty.
- `malay wedding` returns 4,188 results that conflate Malaysian, Indonesian,
  Filipino and Bruneian imagery, with Balinese ceremonies, Aceh and Jakarta mass
  weddings, and generic tropical beach couples in the top set.

That is the same bias as the free libraries, with an invoice attached. I could
not check Shutterstock; it returns 403 to an automated fetch, so I am not going
to characterise its catalogue. Getty alone is enough to say a subscription is
not the answer to these six subjects.

**Do not fill the gaps with Indonesian photography, at any price.** Six of our
28 articles going without a photograph is a smaller cost than one article that
tells a Malaysian couple that Javanese seserahan is their hantaran. The
absence is honest. The substitute is not.
