## The finding that changed the cluster

The verification pass established, from primary sources, that **six of fourteen
Malaysian jurisdictions fix no minimum mas kahwin at all**, and that **three of
the figures dominating page one of Google have no official backing anywhere**.

| Circulating claim | What the primary sources show |
|---|---|
| Perak RM101 | No Perak source states any figure. The 2013 gazette was read in full: 20 occurrences of "mas kahwin" across 84 pages, all blank form fields. |
| Pulau Pinang RM24 | No Penang source states any figure. |
| Kedah RM22.50 | No Kedah source states any figure. It is Johor's number, copied across. |
| WP "janda RM150" and a separate "Labuan RM80" row | Inventions. JAWI states one RM300 floor for the whole Federal Territory. |
| Terengganu RM300 / RM100 | Contradicted on the record by the state's own Pesuruhjaya, Utusan, 29 Mac 2024. |

**This is the cluster's competitive weapon, and it reshaped five of the eight
briefs.** Each state article now carries the figure *or the sourced absence of
one*, plus **the state's official fee schedule**, which no competitor publishes
alongside mas kahwin at all.

## Late rulings, after the boards closed

Four things landed from seats still in flight when the boards reported. Three
were already superseded by the closing verification pass; one was real.

**RULED — the pillar-wide verb. `dibayar`, not `dijelaskan`.** Seven articles
shipped `wajib dijelaskan`, one shipped `wajib dibayar`, and one shipped
`pemberian wajib`. Three forms of a sentence that is supposed to be the house
definition. The verification seat counted both verbs across the four enactments
it had read: **`dibayar` appears 33 to 34 times in each and is the verb of the
s.2 definition; `dijelaskan` appears exactly once in each, always in s.21(2)(c),
the arrears provision, meaning to discharge an amount left outstanding.**

The deciding constraint is framing, not taste. Every one of these sentences
attributes the definition to s.2 in the same breath. A house sentence may prefer
a different verb for readability, but then it must read as our own wording and
must not sit beside a citation to s.2. Ours does sit there. **All eight articles
now read `bayaran yang wajib dibayar oleh suami kepada isteri`.** Recorded with
its reasoning in voice notes §5.10.1, so the next writer inherits why and does
not re-borrow the arrears verb.

Two scope disciplines held: **Sabah 2004 was excluded from the count** because it
is an English-language enactment with no Malay verb to count, and counting it
would have been inventing evidence. And **Perak is a named gap.** Nobody has read
the Enakmen UUKI (Perak) 2004 — the state judiciary publishes the rules made
under it but not the enactment, and all three fallback routes were down. Matching
section numbering across states is consistent with Perak following the same model
enactment; **it is not proof its s.2 uses the same words, and no article claims
it does.** A5 names Pulau Pinang 2004, Melaka 2002 and Kedah 2008 as what was
checked and claims nothing about Perak's own text. Four states read, nine unread,
one named gap.

## Two structural gaps in the workflow, now fixed

**1. Concurrent boards break the blocking seat.** Seven `managing-editor`
instances were live at once, so the role name resolved to nobody and **the
verification seat could not reach its own chair to deliver a block.** That is not
a cost problem, it is a correctness one: blocking authority is the core safety
mechanism of this workflow and concurrency severed it. One chair sat waiting on
answers already produced; the same rulings were re-transmitted three times.

**Fixed in the workflow doc, Stage 4, Rule 7.** Stages 1 to 3 stay parallel —
that is where the throughput actually came from, eight drafts in two waves, and
writers on separate briefs have no seat-to-seat traffic. **Stage 4 runs
sequentially, one chair and one verification seat at a time.**

**2. No board sees more than its own article.** The verb fork existed across
seven articles and **a writer caught it, not a board**, because no board had
visibility to catch it. **A pillar-level consistency pass is now required before
anything in a pillar publishes** — one reader, all articles, checking house
definitions and shared citations agree. Added to the workflow doc.

## Recurring failure modes — fix these UPSTREAM, in the brief

This is the learning loop. Each of these was caught in more than one room, which
means the brief is wrong, not the writer.

1. **A brief may not assert a checkable claim about a competitor's current page without a same-day check.** Caught in A1, again in A3, and the A6 writer caught it before the room did. **Three articles, one defect, and it was mine.** New rule for C2.4's siblings: no sentence about what other pages show, at all, in this cluster.
2. **A brief may not state a source more firmly than the source states itself.** A1 and A3 both inherited a summary that had hardened into a fact. The Johor-to-Pahang transfer is an `andaian` in the paper and was stated as settled in my brief.
3. **A quoted gazette form field is cited by form number AND printed title, verified against the instrument.** A1, A2, A5 and A8 all carried a mislabelled form. One brief defect, four articles.
4. **A brief may not mandate a quotation without checking whether a sibling already took it.** The al-Fiqh al-Manhaji min/max quote was mandated three times.
5. **Where a fee or rate source is a graphic or poster-style PDF, the source step renders it and reads the render.** Text extraction is not sufficient. Two Stage 2 passes made this error and it hid a wrong floor inside a widened band. **A band is how a draft hides an unresolved read.**
6. **A `last fetched` stamp belongs on each figure, not each document.** Pahang's RM45 was Grade A in the morning and gone by the afternoon.
7. **The duplicated duit hantaran instruction** appeared in A1, A4 and A6. Template artefact: voice notes §2.4 move 4 and §4.2 both want to say it. Amended.
8. **Where a brief commissions a cultural section inside a procedural article, it names the terms it expects and caps them.** A6's adat perpatih section ran six Tier B terms against a budget of two, and the room had to restructure downstream.
9. **Brief §2's fee-schedule requirement cannot be met for every state.** Johor publishes none. The brief must say what an article does when the schedule does not exist, rather than leaving each writer to decide whether it is a hole or a finding.
10. **Brief §3.1 must state the distinction itself:** printing a figure *as the state's rate* versus naming it *inside its own refutation*. A5's brief named RM101 four times without settling it, the writer took the safe reading and lost the page's strongest sentence, and the room spent a ruling recovering it.
