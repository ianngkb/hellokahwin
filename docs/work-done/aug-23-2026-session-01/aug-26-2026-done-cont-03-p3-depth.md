## The hukum-versus-adat lines actually drawn

This is the pillar's differentiator and it is the reason both articles exist.

### A4 — `walimatul-urus`

- **Hukum:** akad nikah makes the marriage valid, and walimah is not part of it.
  Holding a walimah is `sunat muakkad` on the groom. Attending a personally
  addressed invitation is `fardu ain` on the Syafie majority view.
- **Adat:** persandingan, adat pikul, candles, kompang. Named as custom, with
  the Negeri Sembilan department's conditions attached to each.
- **Neither, and said so plainly:** pelamin, the number of dulang hantaran, the
  colour theme, the guest count. None appears in any ruling on the page.
- **Madhhab variance carried, not flattened:** three positions in a table, with
  al-Nawawi's fardu kifayah reasoning stated rather than summarised away.
- **Office-to-office variance carried:** Selangor 2025 and Wilayah Persekutuan
  2019 give different lists of accepted excuses. Both are printed, both dated,
  and the article says the gap is a six-year gap and not a disagreement.
- **The near-myth corrected:** *awlim walaw bi syah* is a floor that already
  suffices, not a rate to reach.
- **The honest "nobody defines this":** there is no official distinction between
  `walimatul urus` and `kesyukuran perkahwinan`. The article says so instead of
  inventing one, which is what the ranking Facebook post does.

### A5 — `skrip-pengacara-majlis-perkahwinan`

- **Hukum:** only the akad. Handled by the jurunikah, not the MC; the ijab kabul
  cannot be read off a script or shortened for time.
- **Adat:** the welcome performances, bersanding, merenjis. Marked in the
  running-order table's third column.
- **Administrative protocol, which is neither:** the JAKIM doa timing, the
  Susunan Keutamaan, the panggilan hormat pekeliling, the colour taboos. Each is
  stated as binding the majlis it was written for, followed by an explicit line
  that it does not bind the reader's kenduri.
- **The running order's honest provenance:** the third column says "Kebiasaan
  setempat" nine times where that is the truth. Nothing in the aturcara is
  presented as a rule.
- **The specimen wording is labelled ours**, in the section's first sentence,
  with the statement that no official wedding text exists.
- **State named where the source names one:** ungu for Kerabat Diraja Perak,
  songket for Kerabat Diraja Negeri Sembilan.

## What was deliberately left out, and why

1. **All Arabic script.** Both articles carry zero Arabic characters.
   `walimah al-urus` appears as transliteration only. The style guide permits
   script once in a definitional article, and A4 qualifies, but the DBP entry
   renders the term without a word space and I would have been reproducing a
   glyph string I could not proofread. A garbled lafaz is worse than none, and
   the value here was near zero.
2. **The hadith numbers where two official offices disagree.** Selangor cites
   the Ibn Umar hadith as Sahih Muslim 1429; Wilayah Persekutuan cites it as
   1053. Both were reporting the same hadith. A4 names narrator and collection
   and skips the number. The Anas bin Malik hadith carries its number because
   only one office cited it, and the article says so.
3. **When a walimah should be held**, before or after consummation. There is a
   genuine scholarly difference and **no Malaysian official source could be
   found stating a position**. Left out entirely rather than sourced to a
   general reference.
4. **Whether a woman may be a wedding MC.** Real question, real demand, and no
   Malaysian fatwa or departmental answer found on it. The nearest citable
   material is about singing and syair, which is a different question. Flagged
   here rather than answered. **This is a gap worth a brief of its own.**
5. **The full 67-line Susunan Keutamaan and the full panggilan hormat tables.**
   Four lines of each are in A5. The rest is federal-ceremony material and would
   have turned a wedding article into a protocol manual.
6. **The Perak Mufti copy of the older doa guideline.** The PDF at
   `mufti.perak.gov.my` returns 404 as of 25 Ogos 2026. Not cited.
7. **A `Kesimpulan` section on either article.** Both close on a next step.

Nothing in either article is a figure, rate, fee, date or source I could not put
a named authority behind. No hadith, source or attribution was invented.

### 1. What did we learn that is not written down

**P3 keeps producing a rule that is neither hukum nor adat, and the guide has
only two boxes.** JAKIM's two-to-three-minute doa is not a religious obligation.
The Susunan Keutamaan is not adat. The panggilan hormat pekeliling is neither.
All three are government administrative protocol, they bind a majlis rasmi
kerajaan, and they bind nothing at a kenduri.

Three articles have now each invented their own phrasing for this distinction.
`P3-A3` wrote "Yang mana tuntutan agama, yang mana urusan majlis" in Ogos. Both
CONT-03 articles reached for it again today and wrote it two more ways. Three
inventions of the same idea is a missing standard, not three writing decisions.

The failure mode it prevents is specific and bad: an article that quotes JAKIM's
timing without saying who JAKIM's document binds reads as though JAKIM regulates
your kenduri. That is a factual error wearing the clothes of helpfulness, and it
is exactly the kind of thing this pillar exists to stop other sites doing.

**Second thing, smaller:** the SERP for both of these keywords is held entirely
by document hosts and dictionaries. Scribd, SlideShare, AnyFlip, Studocu,
Wikipedia, DBP, Shutterstock, a Google Groups thread from someone's 2008 wedding
invitation. Not one publisher on either page one. The competitive read for P3
C3.3 and C3.4 is not "beat the incumbent". It is "be the first publisher to
turn up".
