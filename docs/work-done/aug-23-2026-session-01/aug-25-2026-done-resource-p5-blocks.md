## 2. `C5-1-A1-pelamin` — the honest negative, and what forced it

### What the block was

Six of eight rows in the price table came from `surayarahman.blogspot.com`,
whose archive **ends in May 2014**. The draft disclosed that the list carried no
update date. It did not disclose that the source had been dormant for twelve
years. In an article titled *Pelamin 2026*, the board called that the difference
between a caveat and a misdirection, and they were right.

Suraya Beauty Bridal is now **entirely removed** — table and prose.

### What the search actually found

Six vendor and directory pages read on 25 Ogos 2026. Three publish a figure.
**One** publishes a dated pelamin price.

Corrections the board asked for, all applied:

  floor. RM350 (The Dulang) is now the actual published floor and leads the
  article.
- **gokahwin ceiling resolved.** Displayed 300-pax range **RM9,900** (Laman
  Perkahwinan NMR, Setiawangsa) to **RM29,900** (SkyGlass, Cyberjaya). "Harga
  Biasa" range RM11,900 to RM31,900. Both stated separately, neither conflated.
- **RM1,000–RM3,000 fresh-flower upgrade cut.** That section now carries

### Two findings worth more than the figures

**The directory contradicts itself.** All ten gokahwin rows are labelled
"Diskaun RM2,000", but on Dewan Impiana (11,900 / 12,900) and Dewan Seri Hatinie
(17,500 / 20,900) the 300-pax price shown is **higher** than that row's own
regular price. The article says so and tells the reader to confirm with the hall.

**"Pelamin Sanding Sedia Ada."** That is the verbatim wording in Laman
Perkahwinan NMR's inclusion list. An all-in hall package means the hall's
existing stock pelamin, not one designed to a couple's theme. That single phrase
is more use to a reader than any range, and it is now the point the section
turns on.

### What was withheld, and why the article says so

Two fuller size-by-size pelamin ladders exist and neither is in the article.
One sits on a directory that returns a bot challenge, so it could not be read.
One circulates as a social-media screenshot whose publication date could not be
confirmed. The article states both reasons. Per the verification lead, saying
*why* a figure was withheld is the competitive advantage, not a hedge.

## Retrospective

Chaired by `managing-editor`, written by me.

### What did we learn that is not written down anywhere

**A caveat is not a fix.** The pelamin draft disclosed that its source carried no
update date and published it anyway. Every rule in force was satisfied. The
draft was honest about its ignorance and still wrong, because honesty about a
gap is not the same as closing it. Where a figure cannot be dated, the move is
to cut it, not to footnote it.

**The absence is the article.** Six pelamin sources produced one dated price.
Writing that down honestly, with the reasoning a reader can act on, produced a
better page than the invented table it replaced — and the single most useful
line in it is not a number at all, it is the phrase "Pelamin Sanding Sedia Ada"
lifted verbatim from a hall's inclusion list.

**The failure mode has one shape, and it recurs.** A filtered category page read
as a catalogue. One product page read as a supplier. One page of a vendor's blog
read as the vendor. A URL slug (`50pcs`) read instead of the product title
(`25PCS`). Four different errors across two articles and two review rounds, all
the same mistake: **a partial view of a source treated as the whole source.**
Nobody had named that as one thing before today.

### Which document must change, and who owns the edit

`docs/plans/aug-23-2026-session-01/aug-23-2026-style-guide.md`, **§7.1** — owned
by me, edited today, done.

The existing rule read: *"Every ringgit figure that can change carries its source
and the month it was checked."* That rule is why the 2014 blog shipped. It
records **when we looked**. It never asks **when the source last moved**. A
writer can comply with it perfectly and publish a twelve-year-old figure, which
is exactly what happened.

The standard did not belong in a brief. A brief is read once, by one writer, for
one task. This is a permanent rule about every price on the site, so it went
into the style guide as **§7.1a** and into the reviewer's checklist as **S17**,
non-waivable. The enforcement point went into
`aug-23-2026-production-doctrine.md` as QC **check 5**, because a rule with no
gate is a suggestion.

The verification lead had already written three of these patterns into §7 of
their own work-done log after the first block. A work-done log is a record, not
an instrument — no writer reads one before drafting. That is why the same class
of error recurred within a single day, in the same batch, on the re-check.
**Findings that should change behaviour go into the style guide or the doctrine,
never only into a log.** That is the second doctrine change, and it is the one I
would most want the next retrospective to check on.

### What did we do twice

Verified JV Craft's 49-result catalogue twice — once by the verification lead
in the original block, once by me before republishing the figure. That
duplication was correct and I would do it again; I do not publish a number I
have not read at its origin.

What was genuinely wasted: two rounds on the same class of fault. The
PP Signature RM350 block cost a full review cycle and was avoidable if rule 5
had existed at 09:00 instead of 17:00.

### What did we nearly ship, and what caught it

**Nearly shipped: "The Dulang publishes no price."** False. They publish RM350,
dated January 2025, on a sibling page I never opened. Caught by the
`editorial-verification-lead` on the re-check. It would have been the more
damaging error of the two, because the entire article argues that vendors do not
publish prices — a false negative inside an honest-negative article is the thesis
collapsing, not a detail.

**Nearly shipped: RM5.20 a stem for hand-wound bunga telur.** The URL says
`50pcs`; the product is 25. Caught before drafting, by reading the product title
rather than the slug.

**Nearly shipped: search-engine summaries as sources.** A search result offered
me a complete pelamin ladder (RM3,000 to RM40,000 by size and flower type) from
`wedding.com.my`. The page returns a Cloudflare challenge and I could not read
it. Publishing a figure a search engine reported but I never saw would have been
the 2014 blog error wearing a 2026 costume. Caught by me, and it is why the
article now states *why* both withheld ladders were withheld.

**Nearly shipped: "sepuluh sumber disemak" against a table showing five.**
Caught by `/humanizer`, of all things, on the accuracy pass rather than the
prose pass.
