## 2. The finding that shapes C7.4

**No religious authority in Malaysia publishes a doa for a majlis
pertunangan.** I checked JAKIM, Jabatan Mufti Wilayah Persekutuan, Jabatan
Mufti Negeri Selangor and the state jabatan agama portals. Every result under
that keyword is a wedding blog, a Scribd upload or a PDF-farm copy of one.

So the article does not print one. It reports the absence, explains why it
makes sense (a tunang is a promise, not an akad, so there is no lafaz to get
right), and then gives the reader what actually exists:

- JAKIM's **Garis Panduan dan Himpunan Doa Bagi Majlis Rasmi dan Separuh Rasmi
  Kerajaan, terbitan 2026**, which sets the structure of a majlis doa, who may
  read it, the adab, and a two-to-three-minute cap. I have flagged its scope
  honestly in the article: it governs government ceremonies, and a family
  tunang is not one. It is still the only published Malaysian standard on the
  subject. *(Edition corrected — see §9. The draft originally cited a
  superseded undated edition.)*
- That book's **doa collection**, which in the 2026 edition runs to four doa and
  contains no wedding doa, let alone an engagement one. Two older editions do
  carry a wedding doa and still no engagement one. That absence is the story.
- Mufti WP **Irsyad al-Hukum 954 (16 April 2026)**, which rules on the
  "seperti Yusuf dan Zulaikha, Musa dan Safurah, Sulaiman dan Balqis" doa that
  circulates in every Malaysian wedding blog, and on rhymed doa (al-saja')
  via al-Bukhari 6337.

This is the C2.4 "six of fourteen states fix no minimum" move applied to P3/P7
territory: the honest negative finding is the differentiator.

## 3. Every place hukum was separated from adat

The brief called this the defining distinction. Listed in full so the
Verification Lead can check each one.

### C7.3 cincin tunang

| Claim | Called | Source
|---|---|---|
| Three rings (merisik / tunang / nikah) | **Adat** | JKKN Pemetaan Budaya entries |
| Number of rings, ring-fitting ceremony, who fits it | **Adat** | JKKN |
| Ring on the fourth finger | **Adat**, explicitly not hukum | JKKN; no ruling found |
| A ring as a condition of a valid engagement | **Not a condition at all** | No nas or enactment requires one |
| Men wearing gold, gold-plate, gold alloy, suasa, white gold | **Haram** | Muzakarah Fatwa Kebangsaan ke-52 (1 Julai 2002); Mufti Selangor Taudhih 30; Mufti WP Al-Kafi 953, Irsyad 187 |
| Women wearing gold | **Harus** | al-Nasa'i 5148 as cited in Irsyad 187 |
| Which finger a man wears a ring on | **Hukum: makruh tanzih** on middle and index; sunnah on the little finger | Mufti WP Al-Kafi 587, Sahih Muslim 2078 and 2095 |
| A Malay man wearing the engagement ring on his fourth finger | Neither makruh nor sunnah. Adat. | Stated explicitly in the article |
| Rings returned on a broken engagement | **Statute**, not adat | Selangor Enakmen 2003 s.15; FT Act 1984 s.15 via Mufti WP Irsyad 794 |

The article also carries a standalone hukum/adat table so a reader scanning on
a phone gets the split in one screen.

### C7.1 taaruf

- Taaruf itself is reported as a **method**, using Mufti WP's own definition,
  not as an obligation. The article says plainly it is a kaedah, not a jaminan.
- The eight garis panduan are attributed to Irsyad Hukum 854 and reproduced as
  the department's list, not as our advice.
- **Khalwat is separated into two layers**: the syarak position, and the fact
  that in Malaysia it is a prosecutable offence with a real penalty. Selangor
  s.29 is quoted verbatim (RM3,000 / two years / both), with the state-variance
  warning attached.
- The two Zakaria verses (Ali Imran 38, al-Anbiya' 89) are labelled honestly as
  **doa for zuriat that Mufti WP cites in a jodoh context**, not as doa
  revealed for finding a spouse. Every blog in this market flattens that.
- "Doa jodoh sebut nama" is answered as **permissible**, attributed to Jabatan
  Mufti Negeri Selangor's e-Musykil answer, with the istikharah framing the
  department itself recommends. Not a ruling of ours.
- Istikharah: dream-as-answer is reported as **not required** (Irsyad 342), and
  opening the Qur'an at random as **makruh in the muktamad Syafi'i position**
  (Irsyad 586). Both attributed.

### C7.4 doa pertunangan

- The engagement itself: **a promise, not an akad**, therefore no prescribed
  doa and no doa needed for validity.
- The `Barakallahu laka...` doa is flagged as **for after the akad**, not for a
  tunang. The article says so directly, because putting it in a tunang majlis
  misdescribes the couple's actual status.
- The "zahirkan pernikahan dan sembunyikan pertunangan" hadith is reported as
  **dhaif**, per Mufti WP Irsyad 163, which names al-Dailami's *Musnad
  al-Firdaus* and the unknown narrators. The sound hadith on the same subject
  (Muslim 1412, meminang atas pinangan) points the other way.
- Whether to hold a large tunang majlis: reported as Mufti WP's position, not
  ours, quoted in full.
- JAKIM's doa guideline: scope stated as government ceremonies, explicitly not
  binding on a family majlis.

## 5. What I left out for lack of a reliable source

Each of these was in scope and was cut rather than guessed.

1. **Retail ring prices in Malaysia.** No authority publishes them, and any
   range I wrote would have been a plausible-looking invention. Instead the
   article publishes the one figure that is verifiable: BNM's Kijang Emas
   selling price for 24 Ogos 2026 (RM19,849 per troy ounce), converted to
   ~RM638/g for 999.9 and ~RM585/g of gold content in 916. Both conversions
   are labelled in the article as **our own arithmetic from the BNM figure**,
   with two caveats stated: Kijang Emas carries a minting premium, and a
   counter price is metal plus upah plus margin. The article then tells the
   reader the three questions that break a quoted price into comparable parts.
   I would rather ship that than a fake range.

2. ~~The full Arabic text of the doa istikharah.~~ **CLOSED — see §8.** It is
   now in the article, retrieved character for character from Mufti WP's own
   Al-Kafi #829 page, with that department's own Malay translation.

3. **The Arabic of `Barakallahu laka wa baraka 'alaika wa jama'a bainakuma fi
   khair`.** Sunnah.com returned 403 and no .gov.my page reproduced it
   cleanly. C7.4 gives the rumi and the Malay meaning exactly as Mufti WP gives
   them, plus the Abu Daud 2130 reference, and does not print Arabic it could
   not verify.

4. **The Arabic mukaddimah and penutup of JAKIM's Doa Majlis Perkahwinan.**
   Present in the PDF, but the Arabic font does not extract as text. The Malay
   body is quoted verbatim and the article points the reader to the original
   document for the Arabic.

5. **A verbatim Malay text of section 15 of the Federal Territories Act 1984.**
   AGC's law portal and CommonLII both blocked automated retrieval. The article
   attributes the FT position to Jabatan Mufti Wilayah Persekutuan's citation
   of it rather than claiming to have read the Act, and quotes Selangor
   verbatim instead.

6. **A JKKN entry for peninsular Malay adat bertunang.** Entry 899 ("adat
   meminang dan adat bertunang") kept returning HTTP 500. The two entries that
   did resolve are Kelantan and Sarawak, and the article uses those and says
   plainly that JKKN records this custom one community at a time and the
   entries do not agree with each other.

7. **An officially sanctioned taaruf or matchmaking programme.** C7.1 topic 3
   asks who arranges a taaruf when there is no family or ustaz. I found no
   JAKIM or state programme, and the private apps are unendorsed by any
   religious authority. The article says exactly that, recommends none of them,
   and instead gives the reader the one thing that is sourced: the "orang
   perantaraan yang dipercayai" guideline from Irsyad 854, with concrete
   suggestions of who that person can be.

| Women-only majlis exception | absent | 7.2 (iv) | 7.2 (iv) |

### 9.2 What changed in C7.4

Not a number swap. Paragraph numbers do not map between editions, so every
JAKIM citation was re-verified against the 2026 text and renumbered:

- Duration: 5 minit → **antara 2 hingga 3 minit**, perenggan 9.3 ii → **9.2**.
- Pembaca doa criteria: perenggan 6 → **7.2**, and the modal verb hardened from
  "seelok-eloknya" to "hendaklah". Added 7.2 (iv), the women-only exception.
- Text structure: old 9.1/9.2 → **perenggan 6** (6.1 mukadimah, 6.2 isi, 6.3
  penutup, 6.4 paparan skrin).
- Tatacara: old perenggan 7 → **perenggan 8**.

**One substantive error the renumbering exposed, which nobody had flagged.**
The draft said doa in Malay is "bukan sahaja dibenarkan, ia dijangka", from the
old 9.1 (ii). The 2026 edition reverses this. Perenggan 6.2 (vi): "Isi
kandungan doa hendaklah dibaca dalam Bahasa Arab. Terjemahan teks doa dalam
Bahasa Melayu, Bahasa Inggeris dan bahasa-bahasa lain boleh disediakan dan
dipaparkan di skrin." The current position is that the body is read in Arabic
and the Malay translation is *displayed*, not read in its place. That sentence
was rewritten. It would have been wrong in a way no reader could have caught.

**Added from the 2026 edition, all new and none of it in any competitor:**

- Perenggan 8.1: the doa reader must be briefed beforehand with the date, time,
  venue, purpose, the names of those being honoured, and the tentative
  programme. Genuinely the most useful line in the document for a family
  running a majlis, and the article says so.
- Perenggan 5.3: the majlis at which the guideline says doa should not be read,
  including those with singing, karaoke, dancing or free mixing. Reported with
  an explicit double scope caveat — it governs government majlis, we issue no
  ruling on anyone's family majlis — and framed as the practical reason a
  religious officer may hesitate when invited.
- Perenggan 6.3 and 6.4: the closing structure, and the recommendation to
  display the text and translation on screen.

### 9.3 The spine got stronger, not weaker

The article's premise is that no authority publishes a doa pertunangan. The
2026 edition's collection is only four doa: lawatan Agong, Sidang Parlimen,
perjumpaan bulanan PM, and doa selamat. It has **no wedding doa at all**, where
the two older editions did.

So the negative now holds across three editions of the current standard, and in
the current one the collection does not even reach as far as weddings. That is
better evidence than an absence in an undated file, which is the P3 writer's
point and it is right.

### 9.6 Follow-on edit — the language inference in the closing checklist

Made after the team lead had cleared C7.4, on their explicit approval, and
prompted by a check the P3 writer asked me to run.

P3 asked whether C7.4 leaned on JAKIM permissiveness about language anywhere
beyond the clause I had already corrected. It did, by implication. The closing
checklist "Cara menyusun doa majlis tunang anda" names Arabic for the mukadimah
(item 1) and the penutup (item 4) and names no language for the isi kandungan
(items 2 and 3). A reader infers Malay is acceptable for the body — two screens
after being told perenggan 6.2 (vi) requires the isi kandungan in Arabic. The
article never reconciled the two, so the section a reader actually acts on
quietly implied the opposite of the rule stated above it.

The inference is probably correct for a family majlis. The defect was that the
article never said so, or why. Same self-contradiction shape the team lead found
in C3.2's FAQ, only softer: mine implied where C3.2's stated.

**Fixed by appending one paragraph after the checklist, nothing else touched.**
It states scope only — 6.2 (vi) binds majlis rasmi and separuh rasmi, a
pertunangan at home is neither, so the clause sets nothing for the reader's
majlis — asserts no permissibility, issues no ruling, adds no source, and sends
the reader to their imam kariah or district religious office. No paragraph
renumbering, no citation change.

Two things worth recording about how this was handled. First, I did not edit a
file the team lead had frozen; I put the fix to them as a yes-or-no with the
exact text drafted, and made it only on approval. Second, C7.1 and C7.3 were
swept for the same defect: C7.3 never mentions JAKIM, and C7.1's single JAKIM
reference concerns the absence of an official taaruf programme and has nothing
to do with language. Neither was affected.

**Independent corroboration of the renumbering.** P3 verified that clauses
4.1-4.3, 5.1-5.4, 7.2 (including 7.2 iv), 8.1-8.5, 9.1 and 9.2 are identical in
wording and numbering across the 2025 and 2026 editions, and that only 6.2 (vi)
and the Bahagian 2 collection changed. Every paragraph C7.4 cites sits in those
stable ranges or is 6.2 (vi) itself, so §9.2's renumbering is confirmed from a
second direction rather than resting on my own re-reading.

**One transcription note.** The `skirn` typo inside the 6.2 (vi) quotation is
JAKIM's own and appears identically in both my extraction and P3's. It is
quoted as printed rather than silently corrected, so anyone checking the
article against the PDF finds what we found.

# METHODOLOGY NOTE — for the whole team, not about these three articles

**Recorded at the team lead's instruction, 25 Ogos 2026. This section is
deliberately self-contained so the board can lift it out whole. It is evidence
about how HelloKahwin verifies, not about P7.**

## An automated summary of a primary-source PDF fabricated both a document section and a figure

**What happened.** While checking the claim that JAKIM's 2026 edition drops the
wedding doa, the team lead fetched the PDF through an automated summariser. It
reported back two things:

1. That the document **contains a Doa Majlis Khenduri Perkahwinan.** It does
   not. The 2026 edition's collection is four doa and carries no wedding text.
2. That the doa duration is **"kira-kira lima belas hingga dua puluh minit".**
   That figure appears in **no edition** of the guideline. The 2026 and 2025
   editions both say 2 to 3 minutes; the superseded undated edition says not
   more than 5.

Neither claim is a distortion of something the document says. Both are
inventions, and both are shaped exactly like facts: a plausible section title
in a document that plausibly would carry one, and a plausible duration in
idiomatic Malay.

**What it would have cost.** The lead was checking a correct finding. Acting on
the summary would have meant instructing the writer to revert correct work, and
pushing a fabricated duration into a published HelloKahwin article attributed
to JAKIM. The article would have carried a number that exists nowhere in the
source it cited.

**What caught it.** Two things, independently:

- Extracting the PDF text and reading perenggan 9.2 directly.
- The contents-list check run against a control file: enumerate the section
  headings in the target document, then run the same extraction over an older
  edition **known to contain the thing claimed absent**, and confirm the method
  finds it there. The 2025 edition's `Doa Majlis Khenduri Perkahwinan`
  extracted cleanly at line 596. That is what turns "I did not find it" into
  "it is not there."

## The rule

**For any claim that rests on what a primary source does or does not contain,
extract and read the source, and prove the method finds a positive control. A
summarised read is not evidence about a source's contents.**

Three practical corollaries:

1. **An absence needs a control.** "The document does not contain X" is only as
   good as the extraction. Prove the extraction would have found X by finding
   it somewhere it does exist — a sibling document, an older edition, a
   different section.
2. **Name the limits of the method used.** Text extraction cannot see a heading
   flattened into an image. Say so rather than implying the check was total.
   That is what left this finding open for a page-by-page confirmation, and the
   confirmation is what closed it.
3. **Two people summarising the same document is not two checks.** Both P3 and
   the writer had reason to trust an automated read here. What separated the
   correct answer from the confident wrong one was method, not judgement.

## Why this matters more for HelloKahwin than for most publishers

Every checkable claim in this pillar is a religious ruling, a state enactment,
a fatwa date, or a government figure. A summarisation layer sitting between the
document and the writer will occasionally invent one, and its inventions will
be indistinguishable in tone from the real thing. A fabricated hadith grading
or a fabricated mas kahwin rate is not a typo — it is the failure this company
cannot survive, and it arrives looking exactly like good work.

## Also worth the board's attention: the defect class the edition check could not see

Two writers went at the same PDF hard on the question "which edition is
current" and between them caught a stale duration, a hardened modal verb, a
dropped doa collection and a reversed language clause. Neither would have
caught what the P3 writer found afterwards in their own A1: a clause about the
language of a **spoken majlis doa** being used to justify the language of a
**written guest card**. Correct edition, correct paragraph, wrong kind of
object.

The same defect appeared one level quieter in C7.4, as an inference rather than
a statement — a checklist naming the language for two items and staying silent
on two others (see §9.6). Inference is harder to catch than a wrong sentence,
because there is no wrong sentence to find.

**Edition-checking and scope-checking are different passes.** Neither finds the
other's defects, and the second one only ran because a writer asked a colleague
a question the first pass had not posed.
