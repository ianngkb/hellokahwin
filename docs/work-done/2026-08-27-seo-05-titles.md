# SEO-05 — Titles: the one statistically real zero, and a seo_title drift audit

**Date:** 2026-08-27 (all measurements taken 2026-08-26 UTC)
**Branch:** `feat/seo-05-titles`
**Owner:** head-of-seo-content
**Status:** DONE, with one thing left open that is not mine to close. Five
titles and metas rewritten and live in production, every one verified from live
HTML. Every `seo_title` field audited. Both rules landed in `ceo-memory.md` and
in the persona.

**The open thing, stated up front because it qualifies every "live" claim
below.** `generateMetadata` returns `{}` when its 1.5s deadline is missed, and
that empty result — the root layout's default `<title>` and description — gets
CACHED. It is real, it recurred on these five pages 14 minutes after a repair,
and it is not fixable from the content layer.

**⚠ CORRECTION, and it is mine.** An earlier version of this document said
**39 of 69** articles were serving the default title in production and blamed
SEO-02, SEO-06 and CONT-08's publishes. **That number was wrong by 12x and the
attribution was unfounded.** UX-01 produced a counter-example, I re-read my own
raw data, and the true pre-existing figure is **3 of 69**. The other 36 were
rendered by my own six-wide concurrent sweep — I measured my measurement. The
corrected section below carries what each number actually supports.

**This is NOT funded as a conversion fix, and no conversion is promised.**
Sitewide CTR is 1.65% at average position 20, at or above the published curve.
We have a POSITION and VOLUME problem. Titles are cheap; they are not the
bottleneck. Nothing below should be read as a traffic forecast.

## The `seo_title` drift audit — every field, all 69 articles

**CLAIM.** Exactly one `seo_title` had drifted from its H1. The audit's real
finding is somewhere else entirely, and it is much worse.

**EVIDENCE.** All 69 published rows, read from production 2026-08-26T17:05Z
(`docs/work-done/2026-08-27-seo-05-titles-EVIDENCE/seo-title-drift-audit.json`):

| | count |
| --- | --- |
| published articles | 69 |
| `meta_title` IS NULL | **40** |
| `meta_title` set (all 29 legacy WordPress imports) | 29 |
| - identical to the H1 after brand-strip | 28 |
| - **drifted from the H1** | **1** (`mas-kahwin-ikut-negeri`) |
| rendered title >= 60 chars, before this item | **69 of 69** |
| `meta_description` that is a truncated body fallback ending in an ellipsis | **28** |

Three findings, in ascending order of importance.

**1. The drift count is one.** The brief expected "at least one, total
unknown". It is exactly the one already identified. No hidden stale field.

**2. Forty articles have no SEO title at all, structurally.** Their title is
the H1 verbatim plus the brand. `scripts/ingest-article.mts:810-856` never
writes `meta_title` — not on insert, not in its `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` — so
there is no way to give an ingested article a shorter title than its H1. A
useful side effect: **`meta_title` written by hand survives a re-ingest, while
`meta_description` does NOT** (the upsert overwrites it from the file). All five
articles changed here are legacy WordPress imports with no source file, so
neither is at risk today.

**3. Twenty-eight meta descriptions are not descriptions.** They are the first
~140 characters of the article body, cut mid-sentence at an ellipsis — "…Banyak
dewan komuniti dan…", "…Ramai pasangan terburu-buru…". Four of the five fixed
here were among them. **Twenty-four remain.** Not opened; it is a content pass,
not a title pass, and it should be sized on its own.

## What the audit actually found: a cached metadata failure, and how badly I first sized it

**CLAIM, corrected.** `generateMetadata` returns `{}` on a 1.5s deadline miss;
the page then ships the root layout's default title and description with a
correct database row and a correct `<h1>` on the same page; and that empty
result is cached. **Three of 69 articles were in that state when I started.**
A cold render is not sufficient to cause it — CONTENTION is the discriminator,
and the evidence for that is UX-01's, not mine.

**WHAT I FIRST CLAIMED, AND WHY IT WAS WRONG.** I reported 39 of 69 and
attributed them to SEO-02, SEO-06 and CONT-08. My first sweep was **six-wide
concurrent**, and splitting its own cache headers settles it:

```
the 39 that served the default title, by x-vercel-cache:   MISS 36   HIT 3
  HIT  -> the edge already held a bad entry      -> PRE-EXISTING:      3
  MISS -> the origin rendered during my sweep    -> POSSIBLY MY LOAD: 36

control, the 30 that served their OWN title in the same sweep:  MISS 20  HIT 7  STALE 3
```

Twenty cold renders in that same sweep produced correct titles. So concurrency
raises the miss rate sharply — **36 of 56 cold renders failed, 64%** — without
being deterministic. **The pre-existing damage was 3 articles**
(`goodies-kahwin`, `mas-kahwin-ikut-negeri`, `tempat-honeymoon-di-malaysia`),
and those three are the only ones the sequential STALE re-check ever confirmed.
I generalised from 3 to 39 and did not notice that the check I was proud of
covered one-thirteenth of the claim.

The "51 of 69 immediately after the documented sequence" figure carries the same
confound — that sweep was six-wide too. **It is withdrawn as a measurement of
what `revalidate-content` does.**

**EVIDENCE — the census as it was actually taken.** Six-wide, 2026-08-26T16:58Z,
39 returned:

```html
<title>HelloKahwin — Idea &amp; Panduan Perkahwinan Malaysia</title>
<meta name="description" content="Idea, tips dan panduan perkahwinan untuk pasangan Malaysia. Rancang majlis impian anda mengikut bajet."/>
```

That is `src/app/layout.tsx:8` — the root default — not any article's title.

**EVIDENCE — I checked whether my own load caused it, and it did not.** Fetched
sequentially, 1.2s apart, the pattern is exact: every response carrying
`x-vercel-cache: STALE` served the default title; the first response that was
not STALE served the correct one.

```
goodies-kahwin           DEFAULT/STALE  DEFAULT/STALE  own/HIT
mas-kahwin-perak         DEFAULT/STALE  DEFAULT/STALE  own/HIT
harga-sewa-dewan-kahwin  DEFAULT/STALE  DEFAULT/STALE  own/HIT
```

**MECHANISM.** `src/app/(public)/artikel/[category]/[slug]/page.tsx:429`:

```ts
try {
  pageData = await withDeadline(getArticlePageData(slug), 1_500, `inspire-article-meta:${slug}`);
} catch {
  return {};
}
```

`generateMetadata` runs under a 1.5-second deadline and returns `{}` on a miss.
Next then falls back to the root layout's metadata. **That empty result is
prerendered and cached** (`X-Nextjs-Prerender: 1`), and
`s-maxage=600, stale-while-revalidate=3000` serves it for up to an hour. A
cached failure — the same shape as the `revalidateTag(tag, 'max')` defect one
layer down.

**WHAT TRIGGERS IT — the corrected account, and the best evidence is UX-01's.**
I first said "a cold render loses the deadline". **UX-01 falsified that.** A
production deploy landed ~17:53Z — the most total cache drop available, every
cache cold at once — and she audited every live article 12 minutes later,
**strictly sequentially, one request at a time**:

```
live article URLs in sitemap : 74
correct <title>              : 74
root default <title>         :  0
measured 18:05:49Z
```

Most had not been requested since the deploy, so her curl WAS the cold render.
**Cold is not sufficient.** Her reading is that the discriminator is
CONCURRENCY — N renders contending for the same DB pool miss 1.5s together —
and my own data says the same thing from the other side: 36 of 56 cold renders
failed under six-wide load while 20 succeeded, and a clean sequential census I
took at 18:10:41Z found **0 of 69** wrong (cache mix 63 HIT / 6 STALE,
`…-EVIDENCE/sequential-census.json`).

**A caution that follows, and it lands on my own recommendation.** My repair
routine bulk-purges 69 tags at once and then fetches sequentially — round 1
still left **55 of 69** wrong. If contention is the trigger, **the bulk purge is
itself a stampede**, because deleting 69 entries at once invites 69
regenerations at once regardless of how politely I then fetch. That reconciles
my 55-of-69 with UX-01's 74-of-74: her deploy did not synchronise regeneration,
my purge did. **Stated as a hypothesis, not a finding** — I cannot see the
origin's concurrency from outside, and it should be tested before anyone builds
on it.

**TWO NEGATIVE RESULTS, so nobody spends a day re-deriving them.**

*Page size does not explain which articles are affected.* UX-01 tested the
obvious hypothesis — that the three are the heaviest rows, so only they cross
1.5s — and it fails in both directions. Ranked by page size over all 74 live
articles: `tempat-honeymoon-di-malaysia` **rank 1** (190 KB),
`mas-kahwin-ikut-negeri` **rank 19** (136 KB), `goodies-kahwin` **rank 72 of
74** (91 KB), against a 124 KB median. And the decisive control:
`garden-wedding` is also 190 KB and was NOT affected, while `goodies-kahwin` at
rank 72 was. **So the three are not special** — which supports the contention
reading rather than undermining it. It also means "which articles are affected"
is **not a stable set and must not be treated as a list to fix.**

*An uncontended forced origin render makes the deadline comfortably.* I forced
ten origin renders without purging anything, using a distinct query string as a
distinct CDN cache key, strictly sequential, 1.5s apart. All ten were genuine
`MISS` responses; **0 of 10 produced the default title**:

```
tempat-honeymoon-di-malaysia  3148ms  MISS  190KB  own title
kursus-kahwin                 1868ms  MISS  148KB  own title
garden-wedding                1657ms  MISS  190KB  own title
pelamin                       1643ms  MISS  128KB  own title
goodies-kahwin                1555ms  MISS   91KB  own title
dewan-kahwin                  1478ms  MISS  112KB  own title
rukun-nikah                   1398ms  MISS  122KB  own title
checklist-kahwin              1248ms  MISS  122KB  own title
mas-kahwin-ikut-negeri        1143ms  MISS  136KB  own title
mas-kahwin-perak              1095ms  MISS  112KB  own title
                              median 1555ms
```

Independently reproduces UX-01's single 3.185s cold sample on the same page.
**But read those totals carefully**: they are network plus edge plus render, and
the 1.5s deadline applies only to the DB read inside them, which is a subset. So
they bound the whole request, not the thing the deadline governs.

*And the ordering confound on that 3148ms outlier is UNRESOLVED.* It ran first.
I tried to control for it by re-running the same ten in reverse, and the control
was invalid — every row came back `HIT`, so none was a forced render. Repeating
with verified-unique query keys gave the same. **The query string is not part of
this route's CDN cache key**, so the first probe created the entries and every
later probe hits them; a second forced render is only available by EVICTING,
which is the stampede this work is trying not to cause. I stopped there rather
than assert either reading. Both probe files are in the evidence folder, the
second labelled invalid.

**What I withdraw.** That `revalidate-content` is a proven sitewide trigger, and
that SEO-02, SEO-06 and CONT-08's publishes caused the damage. Neither survives
the confound. `revalidate-content` remains a *plausible* trigger by the same
stampede argument — it drops every article's data cache simultaneously — but no
clean measurement here isolates it.

**WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT.** The workaround was found and proved on all 69: purge
the edge WITHOUT dropping the origin data cache first, so the re-render finds a
warm cache and wins the deadline.

```
round 1: purged 69 paths, ok=true (HTTP 200 in 5 request(s))
round 1: still default after re-render: 0

FINAL: 0 of 69 still serving the root default title
```

Census in
`docs/work-done/2026-08-27-seo-05-titles-EVIDENCE/sitewide-title-census.json`.

**BUT THE REPAIR IS NOT DURABLE, AND I MEASURED THAT TOO.** Fourteen minutes
after the first repair, all five SEO-05 pages were serving the default title
again — from a FRESH cache entry, `x-vercel-cache: HIT` at `age: 44`, not a
stale one. Something regenerated those entries in between; with four live
hellokahwin sessions there are several candidates and I did not establish which.
The second repair produced the sharpest numbers in this document, though not the
reading I first gave them:

```
round 1: purged 69 paths ... still default after re-render: 55
round 2: purged 55 paths ... still default after re-render:  1
round 3: purged  1 path  ... still default after re-render:  0
FINAL: 0 of 69 still serving the root default title
```

I originally labelled those rounds "cold origin" and "warm origin". Given
UX-01's 74-of-74 on genuinely cold sequential renders, **the better reading is
BATCH SIZE**: 69 tags deleted at once, then 55, then 1 — fewer simultaneous
regenerations each round, fewer failures each round. Same numbers, and they now
point at contention rather than temperature.

**So the honest claim is narrower than "fixed".** The five rows are written and
durable. The rendered titles were correct at 17:41Z, and a clean sequential
census at 18:10:41Z found 0 of 69 wrong. They are correct whenever the metadata
query wins its race, and **no action available from the content layer can
guarantee that.** Anyone reading this later should re-check before quoting a
title as live.

The three active hellokahwin sessions were told directly rather than left to
discover it: `hkdocs-cont08-c4`, `hkdocs-cont05-40`, and
`pillars-ingest-redirects-35`, which owns the route. **All three were sent the
corrected figures afterwards** — the first round of warnings carried the wrong
"39 of 69", and told CONT-08 that its five C2.5 state pages were among them,
which the data does not support.

## Retrospective

**What we learned that is not written down.**

Rule 1 was never actually written down. `ceo-memory.md` referred to it as "the
averaged position below" and the file did not contain it — a forward reference
to a rule nobody had typed. It was applied twice in review and recorded zero
times, which is exactly how it came back. A cross-reference is not a record.

The bigger lesson is about where a field audit should look. The brief asked
whether any `seo_title` had drifted from its page. One had. Meanwhile 39 pages
were serving no article title at all, and the field audit could not see it,
because **the defect was between the field and the SERP, not in the field.**
A row-level check cannot see a render-level failure — the same shape as SEO-02's
lesson that a page-level check could not see a link-level `nofollow`. That
shape has now cost two sprints. The general form is worth stating: **check the
artefact the consumer receives, not the input you control.**

And a cached failure is worse than a failure. `generateMetadata` returning `{}`
under load is a reasonable fallback; freezing that `{}` into a prerender and
serving it for an hour is not.

Four things.

*A title item that would have shipped five wrong titles.* If the "after" check

in the data I had already collected. Corrected everywhere it went, including
back to the three sessions I had warned with the wrong figure.

before committing; corrected in place.
