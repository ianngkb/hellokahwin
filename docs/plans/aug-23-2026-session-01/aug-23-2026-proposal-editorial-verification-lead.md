# Hiring Proposal — Editorial Verification Lead (Adat, Agama & Undang-Undang)

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting board approval
**From:** ceo-hellokahwin · **Date:** 23 Aug 2026
**Origin:** board question — *"Is there a wedding expert we should hire to
review and verify how updated the content is and how accurate it is?"*

---

## Short answer: yes, and the gap is worse than it looks

I checked our own system before answering. Two findings:

**1. Nobody independently verifies accuracy.** The quality bar requires that
*"any religious or legal claim names its source and its state"* — but that is
a rule the **writer applies to their own work**. The Head of SEO & Content
runs QC on structure, keywords, links and language. They are an SEO
specialist, not a domain authority on Malay-Muslim marriage law. Nothing in
the pipeline independently checks whether a claim about rukun nikah, taklik,
or Kelantan's mas kahwin rate is actually correct.

**2. Nothing in the system can detect that content has gone stale.** The
maintenance rule reads:

> **R19. Refresh trigger: traffic down materially from that page's own peak.**

That is the only refresh trigger we have, and it is a *traffic* signal. A page
can become factually wrong — a state amends its enactment, a kursus kahwin
procedure changes, price bands move — and it will keep ranking, keep earning
clicks, and **our system will report it as a success while it misinforms
readers.** R18 books an annual refresh only for "year-stamped and
price-bearing pages", which does not cover a mas kahwin rate or a description
of rukun nikah.

We are about to publish ~67,000 searches a month worth of religious and legal
procedure with no verification layer and no way to notice when it decays.
That is the single largest uninsured risk in the plan.

## It is also a competitive opportunity, not just a risk

Our own topical-authority research found that **E-E-A-T is evaluated per
topic, not per domain**. Our core topics — nikah procedure, mas kahwin,
taklik, adat — are the most trust-sensitive category of content there is.

Now look at who we are competing with: **ppsignature.com is a dress shop.
songketdunia.my is a songket retailer. nikahsatu.com is a blog.** None of them
carries any expert-review signal. If HelloKahwin publishes verified,
source-cited, expert-reviewed procedural content and they publish generic
articles between product pages, that is a durable differentiator on exactly
the topics that matter most — and it is very hard for a retailer to copy.

## What the role is

Not "a wedding expert" in general. Three specific functions:

**1. Pre-publish verification.** An independent check on every religious,
legal and adat claim before an article passes QC: is it correct, is it
attributed to the right state's enactment, is the source authoritative
(JAKIM, state religious departments, JPN), and does it report procedure
rather than issue a ruling.

**2. Currency ownership — the missing refresh trigger.** Owns a register of
every claim in our content that can go out of date: state mas kahwin rates,
kursus kahwin procedures and fees, borang and document requirements, price
bands. Monitors the sources, and flags a page for refresh **when the fact
changes, not when the traffic drops.** This is the hole R19 leaves open.

**3. Standards.** Defines what HelloKahwin will and will not assert on
religious matters, how variance between states and mazhab is presented, and
where we must say "practice varies, check with your state office" rather than
answer.

## Scope

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reviews** | P1 Nikah & Undang-undang · P2 Hantaran & Mas Kahwin · P3 Ucapan, Doa & Adab · P7 Sebelum Nikah — every article on the authority track |
| **Also spot-checks** | Venue capacity and price-band claims on directory pages (a different kind of factual risk, same discipline) |
| **Reports to** | ceo-hellokahwin — deliberately **not** to the Head of SEO & Content |
| **Authority** | Can block publication. A verification failure is not a QC note to weigh against a deadline. |

**Why they report to me, not to SEO.** If verification reports to the person
who owns the publishing target, verification loses every argument with the
calendar. This role must be able to stop an article the week we are behind.

## The honest limitation you should weigh

An AI agent can verify rigorously against authoritative sources, maintain the
currency register, and enforce standards. **It cannot be a credential.** A
line on the page reading *"Disemak oleh Ustaz [name]"* — the thing that
actually converts verification into a visible trust and E-E-A-T signal for
readers and for Google — requires a real, named human with real standing.

So there are two separable decisions:

1. **The agent role** (this proposal) — closes the accuracy and currency gap
   internally. Available now, no external cost.
2. **A named human reviewer** — a real ustaz, a religious-department contact,
   or a wedding-industry professional, retained to review and be credited on
   the authority-track pillars. That is a real-world relationship and a real
   cost, and it is your call, not mine to arrange.

My recommendation: **approve the agent role now**, because it closes a live
risk before we publish a single article. Treat the human credential as a
separate decision worth revisiting once the first cluster is live and we can
show a prospective reviewer real work rather than a plan.

## Where this leaves team size

My standing rule is a small accountable team over a large one, so I will be
explicit. This makes **four** proposed roles: two writers, this verifier, and
possibly a visual lead pending that strategy. I would defend it as follows:
the two writers are capacity, this role is risk control on the majority of
our content, and I am deliberately *not* asking for the visual role until its
own evidence is in. If you want to hold at three, I would cut a writer before
I cut verification — a slower plan is recoverable, a credibility failure on
religious content is not.
