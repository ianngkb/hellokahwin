# Voice Notes: P2 — Hantaran & Mas Kahwin

**Workflow step:** P4, pillar P2. Issued to `writer-adat-agama-prosedur`.
**Owner:** `managing-editor` · **Date:** 23 Aug 2026 · **Session:** aug-23-2026-session-01
**Parent standard:** `aug-23-2026-style-guide.md`. Everything in the style guide applies. This document only says what is **additionally** true because of this subject matter.
**Scope:** all 34 topics across C2.1 Hantaran kahwin, C2.2 Hantaran tunang, C2.3 Gubahan & dulang, C2.4 Mas kahwin ikut negeri, C2.5 Nisbah dulang, duit hantaran & etika.

**Why this pillar needs its own notes.** P2 is the only cluster on the map where a single article has to carry four things at once: a figure set in law, a religious obligation, a family custom that varies, and a conversation about money between two sets of parents. Get the voice wrong and the page is either a fatwa we are not entitled to issue, or a price list for a wife. Both are one sentence away at all times.

---

## 1. We report procedure. We never issue a ruling.

### 1.1 The line

**HelloKahwin states what an authority has set, what a source has said, and what families commonly do. HelloKahwin never tells a reader what is obligatory, forbidden, recommended or valid on its own authority.**

The distinction is not about avoiding religious words. Avoiding them would break the owner-level terminology rule and lose the search. The distinction is **attribution**.

### 1.2 The mechanical rule a reviewer enforces

**Any sentence whose verb or predicate is a hukum term — `wajib`, `haram`, `sunat`, `makruh`, `harus`, `sah`, `tidak sah`, `dibolehkan`, `dilarang`, `syarat sah` — must name its authority and, where the fact can expire, its date, in the same sentence or the sentence immediately following. No attribution, no publication.**

That is a search a reviewer can literally run over the draft.

### 1.3 Phrasings that stay on the reporting side

> Kadar minimum mas kahwin di Selangor ialah RM300 bagi pengantin perempuan yang belum pernah berkahwin, mengikut ketetapan Jabatan Agama Islam Selangor (JAIS), disemak pada Ogos 2026.

> Mas kahwin ialah pemberian wajib daripada suami kepada isteri semasa akad nikah. *(Then the source, named, in the next sentence.)*

> Menurut {pihak berkuasa}, {kenyataan}. Rujukan penuh ada di {pautan}.

> Duit hantaran bukan syarat sah nikah. Ia amalan adat, dan jumlahnya ditentukan melalui persetujuan dua keluarga.

> Amalan ini berbeza antara negeri dan antara keluarga. Bahagian di bawah menerangkan perbezaan itu satu per satu.

> Untuk kes anda sendiri, sahkan dengan Pejabat Agama Islam Daerah (PAID) di negeri pengantin perempuan sebelum tarikh akad ditetapkan.

> Sesetengah negeri tidak menetapkan kadar tetap. Dalam kes itu, jumlah ditentukan melalui persetujuan dan disahkan oleh pejabat agama daerah.

### 1.4 Phrasings that cross the line

> ~~Hukumnya wajib anda bayar RM300.~~ *(We do not assign hukum to a reader's situation.)*
> ~~Haram meletakkan duit hantaran yang terlalu tinggi.~~ *(A ruling, unattributed.)*
> ~~Islam melarang keluarga perempuan meminta jumlah yang membebankan.~~ *(A ruling dressed as a fact.)*
> ~~Nikah anda tidak sah jika mas kahwin tidak diserahkan.~~ *(A validity claim about the reader's own marriage.)*
> ~~Sunat memberi mas kahwin dalam bentuk emas.~~ *(A fiqh category asserted with no source.)*
> ~~Sebaik-baiknya anda letakkan sekitar RM5,000.~~ *(A recommendation of a figure. We never do this.)*
> ~~Menurut Islam…~~ *(Islam is not a citable source. A named authority, scholar, enactment or department is.)*
> ~~Kebanyakan ustaz berpendapat…~~ *(Unnamed consensus is a fabrication.)*

### 1.5 The question we get asked and never answer with our own number

*"Berapa patut saya bagi?"* is the real question behind half this pillar. We answer it with a structure, never with a figure of our own:

1. The statutory minimum for that state, with the authority and the date checked.
2. What families in that state commonly do, **only where we have a source for it**, stated as a reported range, not a recommendation.
3. The factors that move the figure, named plainly.
4. The concrete next step: who to ask, and the exact question to ask them.

If we have no source for step 2, we say so and skip it. We never fill it with something plausible. Stage 2 gate of the production workflow.

## 2. Money between two families

### 2.1 Two failure modes, both live in the market

**Preachy.** Mingguan Wanita's *"Duit Hantaran Hak Siapa? Mak Ayah Bukan 'JUAL ANAK'…"* (read 23 Aug 2026) is a column with a moral position, and it works for that masthead. On a reference page a couple opens the night before a family meeting, moralising is useless to them and slightly insulting.

**Transactional.** Several competitors title their pages *Kadar Harga Mas Kahwin* (observed 23 Aug 2026 on `cikgudigital.my` and `nadz.my`). *Harga* frames a religious obligation as a purchase and a bride as the thing purchased. We do not use that word here, ever, and this is one of the few places where a small vocabulary choice is a whole editorial position.

### 2.2 House vocabulary for money in this pillar

| Use | For | Never |
|---|---|---|
| `kadar` | a figure set by an authority (kadar minimum mas kahwin) | `harga` |
| `jumlah` | an amount two families agree on (jumlah duit hantaran) | `harga`, `tarif`, `bayaran`, `yuran` |
| `nilai` | the worth of goods (nilai barang hantaran) | `harga` when speaking of mas kahwin or hantaran as a whole |
| `kos` | what an item genuinely costs to buy or rent (kos dulang, kos gubahan) | `harga` is acceptable here for a physical product a shop sells, and only here |
| `pemberian` | the act (pemberian wajib daripada suami kepada isteri) | `pembayaran`, `pembelian` |

**AMENDED 23 Ogos 2026 by the Managing Editor, at the A2 review board.** The `pemberian` preference above governs **HelloKahwin speaking in its own voice**. Where a sentence is reporting a statutory definition, **the statute's own noun governs.** The s.2 definition across the state enactments reads `pembayaran kahwin yang wajib dibayar`, so the pillar-wide opening formula is `bayaran yang wajib dijelaskan oleh suami kepada isteri`, as adopted by A1 and A2. Do not "correct" it back to `pemberian`. The point of §2.2 is to keep a religious obligation from being framed as a purchase; tracking the statute's own word in the same sentence that cites the statute does not do that, and diverging from it would misquote the law we are citing.

### 2.3 The stance, written out so it does not have to be re-decided per article

**We are writing to the couple who has to have the conversation, not about them.** We report what is set, what is customary, and what the options are. The family decides. We do not have a view on whether a figure is too high, and we do not have a view on whether hantaran should exist.

**Banned in this pillar, in body copy:**
`jangan bebankan pihak lelaki` · `bukan menjual anak` · `elakkan berlebih-lebihan` as an instruction rather than a reported view · `jangan riak` · `ikutlah kemampuan` as advice in our own voice · `zaman sekarang orang dah tak…` · `nampak murah` · `jangan malu` · any sentence starting `Sepatutnya…`

**Banned equally, in the other direction:**
`hantaran mewah` as an aspiration in our own voice · any implication that a larger hantaran signals a better family · price-anchoring on a luxury example without the budget example beside it.

### 2.4 The negotiation section pattern

Every article that touches a family negotiation (C2.4 topic 8, all of C2.5, C2.1 topic 6) uses the same four moves, in this order:

1. **What is fixed.** The statutory figure, the religious obligation, who owns what. Sourced.
2. **What varies, and along which axis.** State, adat, region, family. Named explicitly.
3. **The question to ask.** Give the couple the actual sentence to say to each set of parents.
4. **What to do when the two answers differ.** A concrete tie-break, usually: start from the bride's state's minimum as the reference point and settle the rest by agreement, then confirm with the PAID.

> **BAIK:** Kalau dua keluarga letak angka yang berbeza, mulakan dengan kadar minimum di negeri pengantin perempuan sebagai titik rujukan, kemudian bincangkan duit hantaran secara berasingan. Dua angka itu perkara berbeza dan lebih mudah dirundingkan satu per satu.
> **BURUK:** Perbincangan tentang duit hantaran memerlukan sikap terbuka dan saling memahami antara kedua-dua belah pihak demi kebahagiaan bersama.

## 3. "Practice varies" without giving a non-answer

The default failure mode of this pillar is the sentence *"Ia bergantung kepada keluarga masing-masing."* It is true, it is honest, and on its own it is useless. It is also what the reader already knew before they searched.

### 3.1 The rule

**A `bergantung` or `berbeza` sentence never stands alone. It is always followed, in the same paragraph, by the axis of variation and the reader's next concrete step.**

Three moves, every time:

1. **Name what is fixed.** There is always something. The statutory minimum. Who the mas kahwin belongs to. That hantaran is not a condition of a valid nikah.
2. **Name the axis.** Not "it varies" but *what* it varies by: negeri, adat perpatih against adat temenggung, bandar against kampung, or simply family. Naming the axis converts a non-answer into information.
3. **Give the next action.** Who to call, what to ask, when to ask it.

> **BAIK:** Bilangan dulang tidak ditetapkan oleh mana-mana pihak berkuasa. Ia berbeza mengikut adat keluarga dan negeri, dan nisbah yang paling lazim ialah 5 balas 7 di majlis nikah serta 3 balas 5 di majlis pertunangan. Tanya ibu bapa pengantin perempuan nisbah yang mereka amalkan sebelum anda mula membeli barang.
> **BURUK:** Bilangan dulang hantaran adalah bergantung kepada adat dan kemampuan keluarga masing-masing.

### 3.2 Where a table replaces the hedge

If practice varies across a set that can be listed, list it. Fourteen states is a table. Two adat systems is a two-column comparison. The style guide already says a table beats a paragraph; in this pillar it is the difference between a page that answers and a page that shrugs.

## 4. The three terms, settled now

Eight articles in C2.4 and eight in C2.5 will each want to define these. **They will all use the definitions below, verbatim where possible.** This is a house usage decision, not a per-article one.

### 4.1 The canonical definitions

**`mas kahwin`** *(also `mahar`)*
The obligatory pemberian from the husband to the wife at akad nikah. It belongs to the wife absolutely. A minimum kadar is set by each state's Islamic religious authority. **House term: `mas kahwin`.** Use `mahar` only in the one sentence that establishes they are the same word, or inside a quotation from a source that uses it. Never call anything else mas kahwin.

**`hantaran`**
The exchanged gift trays between the two families. Adat, not a requirement of a valid nikah. **House term: `hantaran` for the gifts, `dulang hantaran` for the trays.** Never `seserahan` (Indonesian). Never `gift` or `wedding gift`.

**`duit hantaran`** *(also `wang hantaran`)*
The cash sum the groom's side gives the bride's side by agreement, customarily toward the cost of the kenduri. Adat, not obligatory, not owned by the same person as mas kahwin, and not a condition of a valid nikah. **House term: `duit hantaran`**, because that is the form Malaysians search (`duit hantaran kahwin`, 350/mo). Give `wang hantaran` once as the equivalent, because that is what the formal and religious sources call it, then stay with `duit hantaran`.

**AMENDED 23 Ogos 2026 by the Managing Editor, at the A2 review board.** These canonical definitions are written in full for the page that **owns** the entity. **On a page that merely distinguishes the term and stops, the function clause is dropped** — for `duit hantaran` that is `lazimnya untuk menampung kos kenduri`. A2 followed §4.1 exactly and was pulled up by the coverage seat under brief §3.7; that was a conflict between two governing documents, not a writer error, and this amendment resolves it. Definition, ownership and obligation status stay on a distinguishing page. Function, custom and practice belong to the page that owns the entity.

### 4.2 The mandatory distinction block

**Any P2 article that mentions more than one of the three carries the distinction before its first H2.** Either the one-liner or the table. Not both, not neither.

**One-liner form:**
> Mas kahwin ialah pemberian wajib daripada suami kepada isteri semasa akad nikah dan ia hak isteri sepenuhnya. Hantaran pula ialah barang yang bertukar antara dua keluarga mengikut adat, manakala duit hantaran ialah wang yang diberikan pihak lelaki kepada keluarga pengantin perempuan berdasarkan persetujuan. Hanya mas kahwin yang wajib.

**Table form** (use in C2.4 topic 2 and C2.5 topic 5, where the distinction is the article):

| | Mas kahwin | Hantaran | Duit hantaran |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wajib atau adat | Wajib | Adat | Adat |
| Diberi kepada siapa | Isteri | Keluarga pengantin | Keluarga pengantin perempuan |
| Siapa yang memilikinya | Isteri sepenuhnya | Penerima dulang | Keluarga penerima |
| Siapa menetapkan jumlah | Kadar minimum ditetapkan pihak berkuasa agama negeri | Persetujuan dua keluarga | Persetujuan dua keluarga |

*Every cell that carries a hukum word still needs its source named under the table. The table does not exempt the attribution rule in §1.2.*

**AMENDED 23 Ogos 2026 by the Managing Editor, at the A2 review board. Two changes, both binding on every P2 article from here.**

**1. The `Syarat sah nikah` row is STRUCK from the canonical table and does not appear in any C2.4 or C2.5 article.** It specified `Ya` for mas kahwin. That cell is not sourceable and the available evidence runs against it. The verification seat read s.11 of the Enakmen UUKI (Pulau Pinang) 2004 live on 23 Ogos 2026: a marriage is void unless all conditions required by Hukum Syarak are satisfied, and **the enactment enumerates none of them.** The word `rukun` appears nowhere in it. There is no statutory route to that cell in any of the three enactments the pillar relies on. That leaves fiqh, where the validity of a nikah with no mahar named is a genuine khilaf whose majority position runs against `Ya`, and resolving it is precisely what §1 forbids us. A one-word cell is also the worst possible container for a qualified position, because it strips every qualification.

The `Wajib / Adat / Adat` row already carries the status claim with its authority, so no reader loses anything. **A2's writer flagged this rather than filling the cell quietly, and was right to.**

**2. The `Siapa menetapkan jumlah` cell must carry a qualifier when the table appears on any page in C2.4.** As written, `Kadar minimum ditetapkan pihak berkuasa agama negeri` reads as though every state sets one. Six of fourteen do not, and A1 publishes that. The table cell is therefore left as-is, and the source line beneath the table carries **`dan tidak semua negeri menetapkan satu`**. No count is printed outside A1, which owns the figure. Raised by the writer at the A2 board; neither the verification nor the coverage seat caught it.

### 4.3 Sourcing hazard

**Never take a definition of these three terms from an Indonesian source.** In Indonesian usage `mahar` routinely covers what Malaysians call hantaran, and `seserahan` is their word for the trays. A definition imported from there is wrong in Malaysia, and it is exactly what a search engine or a translation tool will hand the writer first.

## 5. The traps

Named so the review board can check for them by name.

### 5.1 The ruling trap
Covered in §1. It is the one that ends the company, not just the article. Any hukum word without an attributed source in the same or next sentence is a block, and the chair backs the block.

### 5.2 The wrong-figure trap
**Every incumbent on this SERP publishes different numbers.** On 23 Aug 2026 I read `thekahwin.my/blog/Mas-kahwin-mengikut-negeri`, which gives Melaka as RM300 to RM600 and Sabah and Sarawak as RM1,000 to RM5,000, while `ecentral.my/mas-kahwin-ikut-negeri/` gives single statutory figures on a different order of magnitude (Selangor RM300, Negeri Sembilan RM200, and Johor at RM22.50). They cannot both be right. One is publishing customary ranges as if they were the legal minimum.

**Rule: no figure in this pillar is sourced from another publisher, ever.** The state authority, its enactment, or its portal. If the authority cannot be reached, the cell says `Belum disahkan` with a note on what we tried, and the article ships without that figure rather than with the herd's. This is `editorial-verification-lead`'s call, and it is a blocking one.

### 5.3 The "no fixed rate" trap
Some states are reported to set no fixed kadar. **That is an answer, not a gap.** The cell never contains a dash, a blank, or `N/A`. It reads:

> Tiada kadar tetap ditetapkan. Jumlah ditentukan melalui persetujuan dan disahkan oleh pejabat agama daerah.

A blank cell tells the reader our research stopped. A sentence tells them what to do.

### 5.4 The `jual anak` trap
Whether a high duit hantaran amounts to selling a daughter is a live public argument in Malaysia, running on Threads, in the comments and in the women's press. **We report that families and commentators differ. We do not join in.** The phrase `jual anak` appears in HelloKahwin only inside a directly attributed quotation, never in our own voice, never in a heading, never in a title.

### 5.5 The adat perpatih and East Malaysia trap
Negeri Sembilan's adat perpatih, and Sabah and Sarawak's separate enactments and native customs, are not deviations from a peninsular norm. Two rules:
- Never write `berbeza daripada kebiasaan` without naming whose kebiasaan.
- Never flatten a custom into a footnote on a peninsular article. C2.4 topics 6 and 7 exist precisely so these get their own page, and the all-states article links to them rather than summarising them badly.

### 5.6 The janda, balu and ibu tunggal row
Several states set a different kadar for a woman who has been married before. Three rules:
- **Do not omit the row because it is awkward.** A reader in that situation is looking for exactly that number, and leaving it out fails her.
- **Write it neutrally.** No value judgement, no explanation of why the figure differs unless an authority has stated one and we cite it.
- **Use the authority's own wording in the table** (`janda / balu / ibu tunggal`, as the state publishes it) and the plainer `pernah berkahwin` in body copy.

### 5.7 The shame trap
A large share of this pillar's readers are on a small budget. C2.1 topic 4 and C2.3 topic 3 are explicitly about doing this cheaply and well. Never imply a modest hantaran is embarrassing. Banned: `nampak murah`, `jangan malu`, `agar tidak dipandang rendah`, `supaya tak malu dengan besan`. Write about cost as a constraint to solve, never as a status to manage.

### 5.8 The virality trap
mStar and Mingguan Wanita's viral register (quote-in-headline, scare quotes, moral outrage) is right for their traffic model and wrong for ours. Our pages are references a couple returns to and that we refresh every January. The style guide's clickbait bans (§10.3) are enforced hardest in this pillar, because this is the pillar where an emotional headline is most tempting.

### 5.9 The image-origin trap
Hantaran, dulang and gubahan photography is overwhelmingly the work of small vendors and photographers, and it circulates on Instagram and Pinterest stripped of any credit. **No gubahan, dulang or hantaran image publishes without a named original owner**, per the owner-level rule in style guide §13. `Kredit: Pinterest` is not a credit. If we cannot name who made the arrangement or took the photograph, the image does not run, however good it is. Two further points specific to P2:
- Never illustrate Malaysian hantaran with an Indonesian *seserahan* photograph.
- Al-Quran, sejadah and other religious items appear on hantaran trays. Photograph selection and captions treat them with the plainness they deserve. No jokey caption, no decorative crop that treats a mushaf as a prop.

### 5.10 The definitions-drift trap
Sixteen articles across C2.4 and C2.5 will each be tempted to define mas kahwin, hantaran and duit hantaran in their own words. **§4.1 is the house wording.** The reviewer checks it against the canonical text. Drift here is how a site stops sounding like one publication, and it is the specific thing this document exists to prevent.

#### 5.10.1 The verb is `dibayar`, and here is why, so nobody re-litigates it

**Ruling, head-of-seo-content, 23 Ogos 2026, cluster C2.4.** The house sentence
is `bayaran yang wajib dibayar oleh suami kepada isteri`.

Seven of the eight C2.4 articles originally shipped `wajib **dijelaskan**`, and
one shipped `wajib **dibayar**`. Both are real statutory Malay. They are not
interchangeable, and the difference is what decided it.

The Editorial Verification Lead counted both verbs across the four enactments it
had read in full:

| Enactment | `dibayar` | `dijelaskan` |
|---|---|---|
| Pulau Pinang 2004 | 34 | 1 |
| Melaka 2002 | 33 | 1 |
| Kedah 2008 | 34 | 1 |
| Terengganu 2017 | 34 | 1 |

**`dibayar` is the verb of the s.2 definition.** All four read
`pembayaran kahwin yang wajib dibayar di bawah Hukum Syarak oleh suami kepada
isteri pada masa perkahwinan diakadnikahkan`, word for word, with the only
divergence being a comma in Melaka.

**`dijelaskan` is the arrears verb.** Its single appearance in each enactment is
always the same place, s.21(2)(c), which deals with a part of the mas kahwin
promised but *not discharged* at the akad. It means settling an outstanding
amount, not making the payment.

**The constraint that decided it is about framing, not taste.** Every one of
these sentences attributes the definition to the enactments in the same breath
("takrif itu terkandung dalam seksyen 2..."). A house sentence may prefer a
different verb on readability grounds, **but then it must read as HelloKahwin's
own wording and must not sit inside or beside a citation to s.2.** Ours does sit
there. So it takes the statute's verb.

**Sabah 2004 is excluded from the count** and correctly so: it is an
English-language enactment, so there is no Malay verb in it to count. Counting
it as a fifth vote would have been inventing evidence.

**Perak is a named gap, not a silent assumption.** Nobody has read the Enakmen
Undang-Undang Keluarga Islam (Perak) 2004. The state judiciary's own portal
publishes the rules made *under* it but not the enactment itself, and all three
fallback routes were down on 23 Ogos 2026. **Matching section numbering across
states is consistent with Perak following the same model enactment. It is not
proof that Perak's s.2 uses the same words, and no article may write that it
does.** A5 is scoped correctly today: it names Pulau Pinang 2004, Melaka 2002
and Kedah 2008 as what was checked, and claims nothing about Perak's own text.

**Scope discipline stands: four states read, nine unread, one named gap.**

## 6. Cluster-specific structure notes

### C2.4 Mas kahwin ikut negeri
- **The table is the article.** It appears above the fold, before any explanation. A reader who came for their state's number gets it without scrolling.
- **Fixed columns:** `Negeri` · `Kadar (belum pernah berkahwin)` · `Kadar (janda / balu / ibu tunggal)` · `Pihak berkuasa` · `Tarikh disemak`.
- **Fixed row order:** the house state order in style guide §7.4. Every state table on the site, every time.
- **The year goes in the H1** because the figure is year-bound and the incumbents are Instagram posts and Facebook videos that cannot be refreshed. Our structural advantage here is the booked annual refresh, so the page must visibly carry its date.
- **The eight state articles** carry the same table shape reduced to their own state, plus what the pejabat agama in that state actually asks for. They link up to the all-states page, not sideways to each other beyond the framework's 2 to 4 sibling links.

### C2.5 Nisbah dulang, duit hantaran & etika
- This is the most distinctly Malay material on the whole map and the least fakeable. Write it as someone who has stood in that living room.
- Ratios are written as figures: `5 balas 7`, `3 balas 5`, `7 balas 9`. Never spelled out, never as `5:7`.
- Topic 8 (which expectations are religious obligation, which are adat, which are recent invention) is the highest-risk article in the pillar. It goes to `editorial-verification-lead` **before** the review board, not at it.

### C2.1, C2.2, C2.3
- Listicles here are categorised, not flat. nikahsatu's `8 Hantaran Tunang…` uses eight bare item names as H2s (read 23 Aug 2026), which is a list, not coverage. Ours group items under an H2 per category with an H3 per item, per framework §2 Type 2 and R9.
- Every item carries a real price or price band with the month it was checked. That is the single specific fact the incumbents do not have.
- Our own live `hantaran-kahwin` article opens with `Artikel ini akan berkongsi kepada anda…` and carries an H2 reading `Ratio Hantaran`. Both are fixed on the upgrade pass: answer in the first 60 words, and `nisbah`.

## 7. What I will name in every P2 review

Per the rules of the room, simplification is a required output. In this pillar I will be looking first at:

1. The gap between the top of the page and the number. If the reader's figure is not in the first 60 words, that is the cut.
2. Hukum words without an attributed source. That is a block, not a note.
3. `bergantung` sentences standing alone with no axis and no next step.
4. A definition of the three terms that has drifted from §4.1.
5. Any sentence that advises rather than reports.
6. Any image without a named owner.
