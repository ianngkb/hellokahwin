# The HelloKahwin Style Guide

**Status:** Managing Editor decision. Operative from the first draft.
**Owner:** `managing-editor` · **Date:** 23 Aug 2026 · **Session:** aug-23-2026-session-01
**Layers on top of:** `aug-23-2026-framework-content.md` §2 (article templates) and §3 (the 21-point quality bar).
**Relationship to the quality bar:** this guide does not replace any of the 21 points. It makes points 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 19, 20 and 21 objectively checkable. Where this guide and the framework appear to disagree, the framework wins on *what* is written and this guide wins on *how it reads*. Anything that looks like a genuine conflict goes to the CEO, not to the writer's judgement.

**Who this is for:** a writer drafting, and a reviewer checking. Every rule below is written so a reviewer can mark it pass or fail without an opinion.

---

## 0. The reader, in one paragraph

An ordinary Malaysian Malay couple planning their own wedding. Not a scholar, not a vendor, not an industry insider. They are on a phone, usually at night, usually with one specific question and a family conversation waiting for them tomorrow. They are not reading for pleasure and they will not read twice. Every rule in this document exists to serve that person.

### 1.2 The two decisions the board delegated

**Decision 1: code-switching.** The evidence says English loanwords are normal and unremarkable in real Malay copy, but that the winners code-switch on **nouns**, not on grammar. ppsignature writes *susun bajet*, *pakej sewaan*, *konsultasi percuma*, all Malay verbs with naturalised English nouns, and in the same breath writes *muat turun* and *senarai semak* rather than *download* and *checklist*. mStar's English is modern-technical nouns in scare quotes. nikahsatu is the outlier, dropping a whole English idiom (*go big or go home*) into a Malay sentence, and it reads as a blog post rather than a reference. **Rule set in §5.**

**Decision 2: reading level.** Measured, not asserted. Across the four pages I could measure, body sentences ran 6 to 20 words, clustering in the low teens, with a comma clause break in every sentence past about 16 words. Paragraphs ran two to three sentences. **Rule set in §3.**

## 2. Register: one voice, held

**We write as a publisher explaining a procedure to an adult who is about to do it.** Not a friend, not a magazine columnist, not a religious authority, not a government circular.

### 2.1 Second person

- **House pronoun: `anda`.** Lowercase, always.
- **Banned: `korang`, `awak`, `kau`, `kamu`, `engkau`, capitalised `Anda`.** *korang* is what ppsignature uses to sell dress rental. We are not selling, and a reference page a couple returns to in three months should not read like a shop DM.
- **Budget: `anda` appears at most once per 150 words**, and only where the sentence gives an instruction or a decision the reader has to make. Explanation is written impersonally, as Mingguan Wanita's reference piece does.
- **Test a reviewer can run:** delete `anda` from the sentence. If the sentence is still true and still useful, the `anda` was decoration. Delete it.

> **BAIK:** Sebelum menetapkan tarikh akad, semak kadar mas kahwin negeri pengantin perempuan.
> **BURUK:** Sebelum anda menetapkan tarikh akad anda, anda perlu semak dahulu kadar mas kahwin di negeri bakal isteri anda.

### 2.2 First person

- **`kami` is banned in article body copy.** HelloKahwin does not appear in its own articles. The publication is named only in a credit, byline or disclosure line.
- **`kita` is banned as an editorial voice.** *Kita akan membincangkan*, *mari kita lihat*, *kita semua tahu* do not ship. They are the single loudest tell of a translated or machine-written Malay blog, and I observed all three patterns on thekahwin.my today.
- **`kita` is allowed only once per article, at most**, when it names a situation genuinely shared by reader and writer as Malaysians, and never as a transition. In practice this almost never earns its place. Default to not using it.

### 2.3 `pihak`

- **Keep it where it is domain vocabulary.** *Pihak lelaki* and *pihak perempuan* are the correct and searched Malay terms for the two families. Every source uses them. So do we.
- **Ban it as bureaucratic filler:** *pihak kami*, *pihak berkenaan*, *pihak yang terlibat*, *pihak tuan*. If it can be replaced by naming the actual party, name the actual party.

> **BAIK:** Duit hantaran diserahkan oleh pihak lelaki kepada keluarga pengantin perempuan.
> **BURUK:** Duit hantaran akan diserahkan oleh pihak yang berkenaan kepada pihak yang menerima.

### 2.4 The register floor and ceiling

- **Floor (too casual):** Mingguan Wanita's viral mode. No *je*, *kan*, *dah*, *tak*, *nak*, *macam mana* in body copy. Contracted colloquial forms are allowed **only inside a direct quotation** from a real person we are quoting, or where the colloquial form is the exact target keyword.
- **Ceiling (too formal):** the government circular. No *adalah dimaklumkan bahawa*, *sehubungan dengan itu*, *tertakluk kepada terma dan syarat*, *sebagaimana yang termaktub*. If a sentence could appear in a surat pekeliling, rewrite it.
- **Correct forms in body copy:** *tidak* (not *tak*), *hendak* or *ingin* (not *nak*), *sudah* or *telah* (not *dah*), *bagaimana* (not *macam mana*, unless it is the target keyword).

## 3. Reading level, sentence and paragraph length

These are numbers. A reviewer checks them by counting.

| Rule | Number | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| Average sentence length across the article | **12 to 16 words** | Fail if the article average is above 18 |
| Hard sentence ceiling | **25 words**, and any sentence over 16 words carries a comma clause break | Fail on any sentence over 25 words with no clause break |
| Sentences per paragraph | **1 to 3** | Fail on any paragraph of 4 or more |
| Words per paragraph | **maximum 55** | Fail above 55. This is a phone screen rule, not an aesthetic one |
| Clauses per sentence | **maximum 2** | No sentence carries three or more subordinate clauses |
| Words of three or more syllables | Prefer the shorter word where one exists with the same meaning | *guna* over *penggunaan*, *beri* over *pemberian* where the verb works |
| Passive constructions | **Under 25% of sentences.** Malay passives (*di-*, *ter-*) are natural and are not banned, but a page of them reads as officialdom | Reviewer spot-checks any section that feels flat |
| The opening | **The head question is answered inside the first 60 words**, before any background. Framework R10, non-negotiable | Fail if the first paragraph is preamble |

> **BAIK (28 words to a usable answer):** Kadar minimum mas kahwin di Selangor ialah RM300 bagi pengantin perempuan yang belum pernah berkahwin. Kadar ini ditetapkan oleh Jabatan Agama Islam Selangor dan disemak pada Ogos 2026.
> **BURUK (60 words, no answer):** Perkahwinan merupakan satu ikatan suci yang diimpikan oleh setiap pasangan. Dalam menuju ke alam rumah tangga, terdapat pelbagai persiapan yang perlu diberi perhatian. Salah satu perkara yang sering menjadi persoalan dalam kalangan bakal pengantin di Malaysia adalah berkaitan dengan kadar mas kahwin yang ditetapkan mengikut negeri masing-masing. Artikel ini akan membincangkan perkara tersebut dengan lebih lanjut.

### 3.1 Simplification rules with a number attached

1. **A table beats a paragraph** whenever the content has two or more parallel dimensions. Fourteen states with two figures each is a table. It is never prose. Framework §2 already requires at least one table in a Panduan; this rule says *which* content must be the table.
2. **A procedure with more than two steps is a numbered list.** Not a paragraph with *seterusnya* and *selepas itu*.
3. **Cut every sentence that exists to sound thorough.** Named offenders: *Perlu diingatkan bahawa*, *Seperti yang kita sedia maklum*, *Adalah penting untuk difahami bahawa*, *Tidak dinafikan bahawa*.
4. **A shorter article that fully answers the question beats a longer one that circles it.** Word counts in the framework templates are ranges, not targets. Padding to reach the bottom of a range is a fail, not a save.
5. **One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph needs the word *manakala* or *selain itu* to hold two ideas together, it is two paragraphs.
6. **If accuracy requires complexity, keep the complexity and restructure the page.** Never simplify into vagueness. A rule that genuinely differs across five states gets a table and five rows, not the sentence *ia berbeza mengikut negeri*.

## 4. Religious and adat terminology (owner-level rule)

**The rule, fixed by the owner and not negotiable: religious and adat terms are used correctly, because those words are what people search for, and each term is explained plainly on first use on the page.** Never strip a term out to sound simpler. Never leave it unexplained to sound authoritative.

### 4.1 The two tiers

**Tier A: everyday Malay.** These need no gloss unless the article is *about* them. Using them is normal Malay, not jargon.
`nikah` · `akad nikah` · `kahwin` · `perkahwinan` · `pengantin` · `kenduri` · `majlis` · `hantaran` · `mas kahwin` · `bertunang` · `merisik` · `bersanding` · `berinai` · `wali` · `saksi` · `imam` · `pejabat agama`

**Tier B: fiqh and formal vocabulary.** Every one of these gets a gloss on first use, on every page it appears on. A gloss on another page does not count. The reader arrived from search on this page.
`mahar` · `mahar musamma` · `mahar mithl` · `sighah` · `ijab kabul` · `taklik` · `wali mujbir` · `wali hakim` · `uruf` · `syarak` · `syariat` · `enakmen` · `mazhab` · `sunat` · `makruh` · `harus` · `sah` and `tidak sah` in their legal sense · `adat perpatih` · `adat temenggung` · `sirih junjung`

### 4.2 The terminology budget

**No more than two Tier B terms are introduced in any one H2 section.** If a section needs a third, the section is doing too much and is split. This is the rule that stops a procedural article turning into a fiqh lecture.

**Clarified 23 Ogos 2026, at the A2 review board.** The budget counts terms **introduced in our own voice**. A Tier B term that arrives only inside a verbatim quotation from a source, and is glossed in the sentence immediately after, does not count against the two. The rule exists to stop us lecturing, not to force the trimming of an official quotation — and trimming a quotation to satisfy a house style rule is a small forgery, which §14 already forbids. A2's opening block carries `mahar`, `enakmen` and `Hukum Syarak`; the third arrives only inside the s.2 statutory definition and is glossed immediately, so the block is compliant.

### 4.3 The gloss format

Two permitted patterns. No third.

**Pattern 1, definition sentence.** Use when the term carries the section.
> **Mahar** ialah pemberian wajib daripada suami kepada isteri semasa akad nikah. Dalam masyarakat Melayu, ia lebih dikenali sebagai mas kahwin.

**Pattern 2, inline appositive.** Use when the term is passing through. Twelve words or fewer, inside the same sentence.
> Lafaz **taklik**, iaitu janji suami yang dibaca selepas akad, dibaca di hadapan saksi.

**Banned gloss habits:**
- Arabic script or transliteration stacked into the sentence for authority. *`(Bahasa Arab: مهر)`* belongs at most once, in a definitional article about the word itself, never in a procedural section.
- A gloss that restates the term with a harder word. *Uruf ialah adat kebiasaan yang muktabar* is not a gloss.
- Deferring the gloss to a glossary section at the bottom. The reader does not scroll down to find out what the paragraph meant.
- Explaining a Tier A word as if the reader has never heard it. Explaining *kenduri* to a Malaysian Malay reader is condescension, and the reviewer should flag it as such.

## 5. Bahasa Melayu Malaysia, and the words that give away a machine

We write **Bahasa Melayu Malaysia**. Indonesian-inflected Malay is the single fastest way to lose a Malaysian reader's trust, and it is what a translation engine produces by default because its training data leans Indonesian.

### 5.1 Wedding vocabulary, where the difference matters most

| Never write (Indonesian) | Always write (Malaysia) |
|---|---|
| pernikahan | perkahwinan |
| mempelai | pengantin |
| calon pengantin pria / wanita | bakal pengantin lelaki / perempuan |
| **seserahan** | **hantaran** |
| lamaran | merisik, meminang |
| resepsi | majlis resepsi, persandingan, kenduri |
| undangan | jemputan, kad jemputan |
| fotografer | jurugambar |
| gaun pengantin | baju pengantin, gaun pengantin |
| souvenir, suvenir | cenderahati, doorgift |
| biaya | kos, belanja |
| uang | wang, duit |

**`seserahan` is the trap in this pillar.** It is the Indonesian word for hantaran and it appears throughout Indonesian wedding content, which is exactly what a Malay-language search or a translation tool will surface. It never appears in HelloKahwin copy.

### 5.2 General Indonesian tells

| Never | Always |
|---|---|
| bisa | boleh |
| butuh, membutuhkan | perlu, memerlukan |
| bagian | bahagian |
| kualitas, aktivitas, universitas | kualiti, aktiviti, universiti |
| resmi | rasmi |
| saat ini | pada masa ini, kini |
| silakan, silahkan | sila |
| kamu, Anda (capitalised) | anda |
| terjangkau | berpatutan, mampu milik |
| mobil, ponsel | kereta, telefon |
| membahas | membincangkan, menerangkan |

I saw `bagian` used for `bahagian` in machine-generated Malay summary text on 23 Aug 2026 while researching this cluster. It is a live failure mode on our own subject matter, not a hypothetical.

### 5.3 Malaysian usage rules that are ours, not Indonesia's

- **`ia`, not `ianya`.** ppsignature writes *Ianya bergantung kepada persetujuan* (observed 23 Aug 2026). We write *Ia bergantung kepada persetujuan*.
- **`di` spacing.** Preposition separated: *di Selangor*, *di pejabat agama*, *di mana*. Prefix joined: *ditetapkan*, *diberikan*, *disemak*.
- **`ke` spacing.** Same rule: *ke pejabat agama*, but *kepada*, *keputusan*.
- **`tip`, not `tips`.** Malay does not pluralise by adding s.
- **`nisbah`, not `ratio`.** Our own live article currently carries an H2 reading *Ratio Hantaran*. That is a fix, not a preference.
- **Reduplication with a hyphen:** *barang-barang*, *kadar-kadar*. Never *barang2*.

## 6. Code-switching: the rule

**The principle, derived from ppsignature and mStar on 23 Aug 2026: switch on nouns, never on grammar.**

### 6.1 Keep the English word when

1. It is the word Malaysians actually say and the Malay equivalent is not in daily use: **bajet**, **pakej**, **venue**, **wedding planner**, **doorgift**, **online**, **email**, **website**, **checklist** *(but see 6.2)*.
2. It is a brand, a product name, a platform, or an official name: *Fossil*, *Shopee*, *Instagram*, *Jabatan Agama Islam Selangor*.
3. It is a technical or modern noun with no settled Malay form: *smartwatch*, *QR code*, *e-wallet*.
4. It is inside a direct quotation from a real person.

### 6.2 Use the Malay word when one is in ordinary use

Even where the English word exists in Malaysian speech, prefer Malay where the Malay is genuinely common. ppsignature does this and it is the reason its pages read as Malaysian rather than as translated. House choices:

| Not this | This |
|---|---|
| download | muat turun |
| checklist (in body copy) | senarai semak |
| free | percuma |
| ratio | nisbah |
| tray | dulang |
| budget-friendly | berpatutan, mampu milik |
| location | lokasi *(already naturalised, fine)* |
| tips | tip, panduan, petua |

`checklist` is permitted **only** where it is the exact target keyword (*checklist kahwin*, 800/mo). Elsewhere, *senarai semak*.

### 6.3 Never switch on

- **Verbs.** No *confirm dulu*, *booking venue*, *survey harga*, *update senarai*. Write *sahkan*, *tempah venue*, *bandingkan harga*, *kemas kini senarai*.
- **Connectors and grammar.** No *so*, *anyway*, *basically*, *by the way*, *plus*.
- **Whole English clauses or idioms.** nikahsatu's *"Jadi apa kata anda go big or go home"* is exactly what we do not do. It reads as a blog, and this is a reference publication.
- **Emphasis.** No English word used purely to sound smart: *seamless*, *elegant vibes*, *journey anda*.

### 6.4 The density cap

**No more than one English-origin word per sentence, and no more than roughly one per 40 words across the article**, excluding brand names, official names and target keywords. A reviewer counts the ones in a random 200-word stretch.

> **BAIK:** Bajet hantaran biasanya bergantung pada bilangan dulang yang dipersetujui kedua-dua keluarga.
> **BURUK:** Budget untuk hantaran anda usually depend pada berapa banyak tray yang both families agree.

## 7. Numbers, ringgit, dates and state names

### 7.1 Ringgit

- **`RM300`.** No space between RM and the figure. No decimals on a whole number.
- **Keep sen where the real figure has sen: `RM22.50`.** Rounding a statutory figure is a factual error, not a style choice.
- **Thousands with a comma: `RM1,000`, `RM12,500`.** Never `RM1000`, never `RM1.000`.
- **Ranges in prose use `hingga`: `RM300 hingga RM500`.** In a table cell, `RM300 – RM500` is permitted.
- **Never `harga` for mas kahwin or hantaran.** See the P2 voice notes. Use `kadar` for a figure set by an authority, `jumlah` for an agreed amount, `nilai` for the worth of goods, `kos` for what something costs to buy.
- **Every ringgit figure that can change carries its source and the month it was checked**, either in the sentence or in a `Disemak` line under the table. Quality bar point 6, made concrete.

### 7.2 Other numbers

- **One to nine in words** when counting ordinary things: *tiga dulang*, *lima barang*.
- **Figures from 10 up**, and always figures for money, dates, measurements, table cells and dulang ratios: *5 balas 7*, *3 balas 5*.
- **Never start a sentence with a numeral.** Rewrite or spell it.
- **Percentages:** *30 peratus* in prose, `%` in tables.
- **Ordinals:** *pertama*, *kedua*, *ketiga*. Not *1st*, not *ke-1*.

### 7.3 Dates

- **House format: `23 Ogos 2026`.** Day, month name in Malay, four-digit year.
- **Malay month names, always:** Januari, Februari, Mac, April, Mei, Jun, Julai, Ogos, September, Oktober, November, Disember.
- **Never `23/08/2026`** in body copy. Numeric dates are permitted only inside a table column that is explicitly a date column.
- **Currency stamps:** `Disemak pada Ogos 2026` or `Dikemas kini Ogos 2026`. Month and year, not a bare year, because a bare year is what an Instagram screenshot offers and being more precise than the incumbent is the whole point.

### 7.4 State names

**Full official Malay forms, every time. No English forms, no abbreviations in body copy.**

Perlis · Kedah · Pulau Pinang *(never Penang)* · Perak · Selangor · Wilayah Persekutuan Kuala Lumpur · Putrajaya · Negeri Sembilan · Melaka *(never Malacca)* · Johor *(never Johore)* · Pahang · Terengganu *(never Trengganu)* · Kelantan · Sabah · Sarawak · Labuan

**Fixed house table order.** Every state table on the site lists states in this order, every time, so a reader who checks two of our articles does not have to re-learn the layout:

> Perlis, Kedah, Pulau Pinang, Perak, Selangor, Wilayah Persekutuan Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya, Negeri Sembilan, Melaka, Johor, Pahang, Terengganu, Kelantan, Sabah, Sarawak, Labuan

**Authorities:** full name on first use with the acronym in brackets, acronym after. *Jabatan Agama Islam Selangor (JAIS)*, *Jabatan Kemajuan Islam Malaysia (JAKIM)*, *Pejabat Agama Islam Daerah (PAID)*, *Jabatan Pendaftaran Negara (JPN)*.

## 8. Headings

### 8.1 Case

**Sentence case. Capitalise the first word and proper nouns only.**

> **BAIK:** Kadar mas kahwin mengikut negeri
> **BAIK:** Apa beza mas kahwin dan duit hantaran?
> **BURUK:** Kadar Mas Kahwin Mengikut Negeri Di Malaysia
> **BURUK:** APA ITU MAS KAHWIN?

Every wedding blog in this space uses Title Case. Malaysian news publishers use sentence case, which is what I observed across the mStar homepage on 23 Aug 2026. Sentence case is how we look like a publication rather than a shop blog, and it costs nothing in search.

### 8.2 How an H2 is phrased

An H2 is one of exactly two shapes.

**Shape 1, the reader's question, ending in a question mark.** Use the interrogative a Malaysian actually types: *Berapa*, *Apa*, *Siapa*, *Bila*, *Bagaimana*, *Bolehkah*, *Perlukah*, *Adakah*.

> Berapa kadar mas kahwin di Johor?
> Siapa yang berhak ke atas duit hantaran?
> Bolehkah mas kahwin dibayar dengan barang?

**Shape 2, the noun phrase naming the thing.** Use for sections that are a table, a list or a stage of a procedure.

> Kadar mas kahwin mengikut negeri
> Beza mas kahwin, hantaran dan duit hantaran
> Langkah menetapkan jumlah duit hantaran

**Rules for both shapes:**
- **60 characters maximum.**
- Contains the entity word the section is about. A reader scanning headings on a phone must be able to find their answer without reading a paragraph.
- **No stacked colons.** *Mas kahwin: panduan lengkap: semua yang anda perlu tahu* does not ship.
- **No decorative headings.** *Pengenalan*, *Mukadimah*, *Penutup*, *Kesimpulan*. If the section is a conclusion, its H2 names what the reader does next. Quality bar point 12.
- **Never** *Tips dan Trik*, *Fakta Menarik*, *Tahukah Anda?*
- H3s nest under an H2 that they are genuinely a sub-type of. Framework R9.

### 8.3 The question-mark rule

If the H2 is a question, the **first sentence underneath answers it**. Not the second, not after a paragraph of context. This is what wins the featured snippet and the AI Overview, and it is the single highest-leverage structural habit in the guide.

## 9. The Soalan Lazim block

The format is fixed. Eight articles must not each invent one.

- **H2 reads exactly `Soalan lazim`.** Sentence case, no year, no keyword stuffing.
- **3 to 5 questions.** Fewer than 3 is not a block. More than 5 means the questions belonged in the body.
- **Each question is an H3**, phrased the way a searcher types it, ending in `?`.
- **No question duplicates an H2 already on the page.** If it does, one of them is redundant and the reviewer says which.
- **Each answer is 40 to 60 words.** The **first sentence answers directly**, and where the answer is yes or no it starts with `Ya.` or `Tidak.` This is exactly what ecentral.my does on the page that outranks us today.
- **Every answer that contains a religious, legal or price claim names its source in the answer.** No exceptions for brevity.
- **No question invented to pad.** Questions come from the brief's Tier-3 question list or from the SERP's People Also Ask. If the brief has no question list, that is a brief problem and goes back to `head-of-seo-content`.
- **Marked up as FAQ schema** by the engineer at ingest. The writer's job is the shape; the markup is not.

> **BAIK**
> ### Adakah mas kahwin sama dengan duit hantaran?
> Tidak. Mas kahwin ialah pemberian wajib daripada suami kepada isteri semasa akad nikah, dan ia menjadi hak isteri sepenuhnya. Duit hantaran pula ialah amalan adat Melayu, diberikan kepada keluarga pengantin perempuan berdasarkan persetujuan dua keluarga, dan ia bukan syarat sah nikah.

> **BURUK**
> ### Soalan lazim tentang mas kahwin di Malaysia yang ramai orang tanya
> Sebenarnya ramai yang keliru tentang perkara ini. Mari kita lihat dengan lebih dekat.

## 10. Titles and meta descriptions

### 10.1 Titles

**Measured on 23 Aug 2026**, the three titles ranking on our head term run 47, 52 and 59 characters, and all three put the exact head term first.

- **50 to 60 characters.** Hard ceiling 60.
- **The exact Malay target keyword comes first**, before any qualifier. Quality bar point 8.
- **Add the year only when the figure or rule is year-bound.** `2026` belongs on a mas kahwin rates page. It does not belong on an article about what hantaran means.
- **One concrete promise after the keyword**, naming what the page settles. Not an adjective about the page.
- **Sentence case**, per §8.1, except where a proper noun or the year requires otherwise.
- **Honest counts in listicles.** If the honest list is 14 items, the title says 14. Framework §2 Type 2.

> **BAIK:** Kadar mas kahwin ikut negeri 2026: senarai penuh 16 negeri *(58 chars)*
> **BURUK:** Mas Kahwin Mengikut Negeri 2026: Semak Kadar Terkini Sebelum Nikah, Jangan Keliru Dengan Wang Hantaran *(101 chars, truncated in the SERP to a fragment)*

### 10.2 Meta descriptions

- **Maximum 155 characters. Counted, not estimated.** Quality bar point 9.
- **In Malay. Describes the page. Does not advertise it.**
- **Contains the single most specific thing on the page:** the number, the state, the year, the count.
- **Contains the target keyword naturally**, usually in the first half.
- **No call to action, no exclamation mark, no ellipsis cliffhanger.**

> **BAIK:** Senarai kadar minimum mas kahwin bagi setiap negeri di Malaysia, disemak Ogos 2026, berserta beza mas kahwin dengan duit hantaran. *(133 chars)*
> **BURUK:** Nak tahu berapa mas kahwin negeri anda? Jangan sampai tersalah! Klik untuk baca panduan lengkap yang wajib anda tahu sebelum nikah!

### 10.3 What clickbait does, and why we do not

Clickbait converts a scroll into a click on social, once. It does not build a page a couple returns to, it does not survive a refresh cycle, and it makes an accuracy claim look like a sales claim. Named bans in titles and metas:

`Rupanya…` · `Jangan terkejut` · `Anda tidak akan percaya` · `WAJIB TAHU` · `Beza tau!` · `Ini rahsianya` · any ALL CAPS word · any `!` · any `?!` · scare quotes around a normal word for effect · a number that is not the real count.

## 11. Warm without being a brochure

Warmth here is a set of behaviours, not a feeling. Six operational rules.

1. **Warmth is naming the reader's real situation, not expressing sympathy about it.** One clause, at the top of a section, then the answer. Maximum one such clause per H2.
   > **BAIK:** Kalau dua keluarga letak angka yang berbeza, mulakan dengan kadar minimum negeri pengantin perempuan sebagai titik rujukan.
   > **BURUK:** Kami faham perbincangan tentang hantaran boleh menjadi sesuatu yang mencabar dan emosional buat kedua-dua keluarga.
2. **The publication never describes itself.** Banned: *panduan terbaik*, *panduan lengkap yang anda perlukan*, *kami sedia membantu*, *sumber rujukan utama anda*. Framework §2 already forbids brochure copy; this names the phrases.
3. **Zero exclamation marks** outside a direct quotation. Counted.
4. **No inspirational close.** Banned: *Semoga bermanfaat*, *Selamat pengantin baru!*, *Semoga urusan anda dipermudahkan*, *Mari kita teruskan tradisi ini dengan bijak dan berhemah* (observed verbatim on thekahwin.my, 23 Aug 2026). The last section tells the reader what to do next and links to the next article in the cluster. Framework §2 Type 1 close.
5. **No moralising.** We are not the reader's parent, imam or financial adviser. See the P2 voice notes §2 for the specific traps in this pillar.
6. **The warmth test a reviewer runs:** does the article name at least one genuinely awkward thing the reader is facing, and answer it? Two families who disagree. Not enough money. Not knowing what to ask the pejabat agama. If it names none, the article is polite but cold, and it goes back. **This is a required output of every review, alongside the simplification note.**

## 12. The banned list

Constructions that mark Malay copy as translated from English or generated by a machine. A reviewer searches for these. Any hit is a rewrite, not a discussion.

### 12.1 Openers and transitions

`Dalam artikel ini, kita akan…` · `Mari kita lihat / selami / bincangkan` · `Hari ini, kita akan membincangkan` · `Assalamualaikum dan salam sejahtera kepada semua pembaca` · `Seperti yang kita sedia maklum` · `Tanpa berlengah lagi` · `Sebelum kita mula` · `Perkahwinan merupakan satu ikatan suci yang…`

### 12.2 English scaffolding translated word for word

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| Adalah penting untuk… | Pastikan… / Anda perlu… |
| Ia adalah salah satu yang paling… | Ia antara yang paling… |
| Dengan kata lain | Maksudnya |
| Sebagai tambahan | Selain itu |
| yang mana *(as a relative pronoun)* | yang |
| di dalam *(for English "in")* | dalam, di |
| menyediakan anda dengan | memberi anda |
| Anda mungkin tertanya-tanya | *(delete the sentence)* |
| Tidak dinafikan bahawa | *(delete the clause)* |
| Perlu diingatkan bahawa | Ingat, / *(delete)* |
| Apabila ia datang kepada… | Bagi… / Untuk… |

### 12.3 Structural tells

- **A `Kesimpulan` section that restates the article.** Banned outright. The close is the next step.
- **Three-item lists everywhere.** If every list on the page has exactly three items, a machine wrote it. Lists are as long as the truth is.
- **Every paragraph the same length.** Vary it. A six-word paragraph after a three-sentence one is good writing, and I observed exactly that on ecentral.my (*"Mas Kahwin dengan hantaran berbeza ya."*).
- **Hedging stacked on hedging:** *biasanya lazimnya kebiasaannya*. Pick one.
- **`sesuatu yang`** used to avoid naming the thing.
- **Em dashes used as a stylistic tic.** House punctuation is the comma and the full stop. A colon where a list follows.
- **Emoji anywhere in an article, heading, title or meta.**

### 12.4 The absolute prohibition

**Nothing publishes without passing `/humanizer`, after review and never before.** Quality bar point 21, owner-level. A draft that has not passed it is not finished, whatever else is true about it.

## 13. Images and credits (owner-level rule)

**EVERY IMAGE IS CREDITED TO ITS ORIGINAL SOURCE. An image whose origin nobody can name does not publish.**

This is how the company runs its licensing. We approach image owners for permission, and a visible, accurate credit is both the courtesy that earns that permission and the record that lets us find them again.

### 13.1 The credit format, on the page

Credit line sits immediately under the image, in the caption slot, as the last element of the caption.

> `Kredit: {Nama pemilik}`

- **Photographer or studio:** `Kredit: Studio Aisyah Photography`
- **Vendor, gubahan artist or bridal house:** `Kredit: Gubahan Dulang Sri Mawar`
- **Venue:** `Kredit: Dewan Perdana Felda`
- **Where a public handle or site exists, the name links to it.** One link, on the name.
- **Multiple owners in one image:** credit the photographer first, then the vendor whose work is the subject. `Kredit: Foto oleh Studio Aisyah Photography, gubahan oleh Sri Mawar`
- **Our own commissioned or owned image:** `Kredit: HelloKahwin`
- **Our own original graphic, chart, table or diagram** *(added 23 Ogos 2026, at the A2 review board)*: **`Grafik: HelloKahwin`.** This is a permitted specialisation of `Kredit:`, not an exception to it — it names the owner exactly as `Kredit:` does, and it is more accurate, because it tells the reader the asset is a drawn graphic rather than a photograph. Use `Kredit:` for photography and `Grafik:` for original graphics. Brief §3.5 and board-passed A1 both already use `Grafik:`; the writer raised the mismatch rather than diverging silently, and this settles it pillar-wide instead of per article.
- **Never** `Kredit: Internet`, `Kredit: Google`, `Kredit: Pinterest`, `Sumber: media sosial`, or an unattributed image. A platform is not an owner.

### 13.2 The record, with the asset

Every asset carries, in the media record, before it can be attached to a draft:

`nama pemilik` · `jenis pemilik (jurugambar / vendor / venue / kami)` · `URL sumber asal` · `status kebenaran (diminta / diberi / tiada)` · `tarikh diperoleh`

### 13.3 Alt text

Descriptive Malay, describing what is in the image, for a reader who cannot see it. **The credit never goes in the alt text.** Quality bar point 14. No stock photograph of a non-Malay wedding on a page about adat, and for this pillar specifically, no Indonesian *seserahan* photograph presented as Malaysian hantaran.

## 14. What HelloKahwin never does

1. Never issues a religious ruling. We report procedure and attribute it. See the P2 voice notes §1 for the exact line.
2. Never publishes a rate, fee or figure sourced from another blog. The authority or nothing.
3. Never leaves a Tier B term unexplained on the page it appears on.
4. Never writes *seserahan*, *pernikahan*, *bisa*, *ianya*, or capitalised *Anda*.
5. Never uses *korang*, *kami* or editorial *kita* in body copy.
6. Never pads a listicle to the number in its title.
7. Never publishes an image without a named original source.
8. Never uses an exclamation mark, an emoji, or ALL CAPS for emphasis.
9. Never closes with an inspirational line instead of a next step.
10. Never sells. No vendor is recommended unless the brief commissioned the comparison and the comparison is sourced.
11. Never ships without `/humanizer`.
12. Never simplifies by becoming vague. If the truth is complicated, restructure the page.

## 15. The reviewer's checklist

The chair runs this in the Editorial Review Board, alongside the verification and SEO seats. It is deliberately countable.

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Opening | Head question answered within 60 words |
| S2 | Sentence length | Article average 12 to 16 words; no sentence over 25 without a clause break |
| S3 | Paragraphs | None over 3 sentences or 55 words |
| S4 | Pronoun | `anda` only; at most once per 150 words; no `kami`, no editorial `kita`, no `korang` |
| S5 | Terminology | Every Tier B term glossed on first use; no more than 2 introduced per H2 |
| S6 | Malaysian Malay | Zero hits on the §5 tables |
| S7 | Code-switching | Nouns only; roughly one English-origin word per 40 words; zero English verbs or connectors |
| S8 | Numbers and dates | RM format, Malay month names, full state names, house table order, currency stamp present |
| S9 | Headings | Sentence case; ≤60 chars; question H2s answered in the first sentence beneath; no decorative headings |
| S10 | Soalan lazim | 3 to 5 H3 questions, 40 to 60 word answers, direct first sentence, no duplication of an H2 |
| S11 | Title and meta | Title ≤60 chars keyword-first; meta ≤155 chars, descriptive, no CTA, no `!` |
| S12 | Warmth test | At least one genuinely awkward reader situation named and answered; zero brochure phrases; zero `!` |
| S13 | Banned list | Zero hits on §12 |
| S14 | Images | Every image carries a named original source, on the page and in the asset record |
| S15 | Simplification | **At least one thing named to cut or make plainer.** A review that names nothing has not been done |
| S16 | Humanizer | Run after revision, and re-checked |

**S15 and S16 are the two that cannot be waived.** S15 because a review that found nothing did not happen. S16 because it is an owner-level rule.
