Parked

One item — and it is the most valuable eight points in the sprint.

SEO-04 — venue entity pages · 8 points · parked

It built nothing, twice, and both times that was correct. The plan was to publish pages for individual wedding venues carrying real prices and capacities. Those figures cannot be sourced — and the second attempt found why, which neither the first park nor the CEO’s diagnosis had identified.

nikahsatu.com is not a competitor we can out-build. It is the operator of those venues. Its own footer reads “© 2019 NikahSatu by Zest Venture Sdn Bhd”, all four candidate venue brands route their bookings through it, and the only published prices anywhere are its own undated discount packages.

So the premise this item was revived on — that we could copy a directory strategy — was measuring a company publishing its own properties. Where it goes next: the page-type decision needs re-deciding from scratch. It must not be revived by changing the sourcing method a third time.

No item was moved out of this sprint. That figure is reported even though it is zero, because until this sprint the number did not exist — and its absence let Sprint 02 record a 100% that described nothing.

The retrospective

Held 29 August with the seats that did the work.

1. What we learned that was not written down

Prose rules do not fire. Gates and scripts do. Everything that worked this sprint was a definition-of-done gate or a runnable script — the sourcing gate that stopped SEO-04 twice, the guardrail checker, a writer producing an undo script before a database write because the DoD said to. Everything that failed was prose in a persona: “resolve the operator”, “re-arm the watcher”, “check the artefact”.

Sprint 02’s retrospective had already recorded that five correct, deployed rules failed to fire. That finding was itself written in prose, and it then happened again.

2. Which documents changed

  • agents/projects/hellokahwin/Executive/ceo-hellokahwin.md — three new sections: the nine bad checks tabulated, the two self-inflicted bottlenecks, and the prose-versus-gates instruction.
  • agents/projects/hellokahwin/Marketing/head-of-seo-content.md — “resolve the operator” moved from ethos prose to a pre-flight checklist item, because the prose version had existed since Sprint 02 and did not fire.
  • skills/startsprint/SKILL.md — re-arming the watcher, and the rule that an authentication stall is a fleet event rather than a per-agent one.
  • docs/plans/aug-23-2026-session-01/aug-23-2026-production-doctrine.md — section 5.7, the rendered-colour audit.
  • Both design personas, edited by the agents themselves and found uncommitted by the closing ship check.

3. What we did twice

  • Nine bad verification checks in one session, all the same error: checked a proxy, with a pattern nobody tested. Two of them would have deleted or reverted shipped work.
  • Watchers not re-armed, three times. One cost ten hours.
  • The competitor rule ignored on a replacement — the exact failure Sprint 02’s retrospective described.

4. What we nearly shipped

Invisible text, sitewide, with every automated check reporting green. A colour-token collision would have rendered gold text on a gold ground across the site. Nothing in any check we run compares a computed colour or a contrast ratio — and the specification carries 47 measured contrast figures that nothing verifies.

It was caught by eye, on one crop, at one screen size, because the affected text happened to sit in the first fold of the first screenshot someone looked at closely. That is not a mechanism. It is now section 5.7 of the production doctrine.

5. What the process got wrong

The sprint was idle roughly three quarters of the time, and the two largest gaps were both the CEO.

  • DES-08 waited 10 hours 5 minutes at a review gate the CEO itself demanded, because the CEO stopped re-arming its watcher. The owner found it by asking.
  • The brand page was held about six hours as an owner decision that never applied — the owner had commissioned that public page by name, with a reference URL. Eleven points sat behind a gate the CEO invented for itself.

Over-escalating and under-watching are opposite errors with one root: the CEO does not model itself as a resource that can be the bottleneck. Three authentication stalls cost about an hour between them — an order of magnitude less.

What held: the review gate. DES-08 offered to ship on “verification is complete”; that was refused, and four named regression checks were demanded with both sides quoted. All four passed against live production. The closing ship check also caught SEO-09 reporting success with a production undo script still unmerged on a branch, and both design agents’ retrospective edits sitting uncommitted.

Filed for next sprint

  • PLAT-13 — the watcher wakes on every curl line and never on a completion. Do not simply delete the pattern: it deliberately fires on deploy status codes, which was the right behaviour in Sprint 02.
  • PLAT-14 — a per-item state-transition log with a “waiting on CEO” state. We can see when work stopped and never why; this sprint’s idle time had to be reconstructed from completion timestamps plus one person’s memory.
  • Unowned: 27 of 48 images on the garden-wedding page carry no credit — an owner-level rule, on the page drawing 28% of all site impressions. And 307 legacy assets still live across the old WordPress articles.
