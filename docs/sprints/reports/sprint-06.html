What shipped

One card per item. Written for someone using the site, not building it.

Six new doa articles CONT-17 · 12pt

What changed. Six new prayer guides went live — for a happy family, the wedding night, moving into a new home, opening a majlis, and for a wife and for a husband. Each carries the full Arabic, a romanised reading and the Malay meaning, and every religious claim names the authority it came from and the edition it was checked against.

How to check it Open hellokahwin.com/artikel/ucapan-doa and look for doa keluarga bahagia, doa malam pertama, doa masuk rumah baru, doa pembuka majlis, doa untuk isteri and doa untuk suami. Each page shows the Arabic, the romanised line and the source under it.

Four new marriage-law guides CONT-18 · 8pt

What changed. Four guides now cover the legal and procedural side of getting married: who may act as guardian, the HIV test, the Selangor pre-marriage course, and the Selangor marriage card. The guardian guide lists all 21 positions in order; the course guide reproduces the full module table and the ten approved organisers with their phone numbers.

How to check it Open hellokahwin.com/artikel/nikah-undang-undang. The site's page count went from 109 to 119 with CONT-17's six.

The article sidebar is finished UI-19 · 8pt

What changed. Long articles now carry a proper sidebar next to the text holding the record panel, the contents list, and — only where the article actually cites sources — a sources block. Articles with no sources keep the sidebar rather than collapsing it, and no empty "Sumber" heading is printed.

How to check it Open any long article on a desktop-width window, e.g. doa-pengantin-baru — the panel sits to the right of the text. Then open garden-wedding, which cites none: the sidebar is still there and there is no empty sources heading.

Category thumbnails are the right shape UI-15 · 5pt

What changed. Thumbnails on the category listings were being squeezed into the wrong shape and stretched beyond their real size — 40 separate cases across the site. They now use a properly cut 4:3 image at every screen width, including on phones.

How to check it Open any category page and narrow the window to phone width. Images stay in proportion and stay sharp.

Every cover knows its own true size CONT-15 · 5pt

What changed. The site now records the real dimensions of all 96 article cover photographs, so a tall photograph can never again be silently forced into a wide slot. It also ships a check that renders each original beside its cropped version so a person can see what the crop kept.

How to check it Covers across the site sit correctly in their frames — browse the article index. The finding that a deliberately tall photograph still loses half its frame is open and carried forward as UI-21; it is not fixed here.

The garden-wedding cover is no longer blurry UI-16 · 3pt

What changed. The page drawing roughly 28% of the site's search impressions was showing its main photograph at a low-resolution size stretched into a bigger frame. It now loads a properly cut image — and a smaller file than the blurry one it replaced.

How to check it Open garden-wedding. The cover is sharp. Measured: 33,574 bytes before, 26,936 after — 19.8% smaller.

A real favicon UI-20 · 3pt

What changed. The browser-tab icon was a pink serif "H" left over from a colour scheme the brand retired two palettes ago, and the standard icon addresses returned nothing at all. There is now a proper monogram in the brand's own cream and near-black, generated from the brand colours rather than being a loose file nobody could regenerate.

How to check it Open hellokahwin.com and look at the browser tab. Bookmark it and check the icon there too.

Headings look like the design says DES-15 · 3pt

What changed. Section headings on the topic pages were quietly ignoring their intended weight and letter-spacing — a stylesheet rule elsewhere was overriding them on every public page. 26 headings across 7 topic hubs now render as designed.

How to check it Open a topic hub and compare a section heading against the same style on the brand page.

A topic page can no longer get stuck empty PLAT-16 · 3pt

What changed. If the database hiccupped while a topic page was being built, the site used to serve a page saying that guide was still empty — and then keep serving that empty page from its cache. Readers were told a fact about our editorial calendar when the truth was a database blip. A failed read now refuses to be published at all.

How to check it Open any topic hub — it lists its articles. A made-up one such as /artikel/plat16-not-a-real-category correctly returns "not found".

The undatable promise is gone COPY-01 · 2pt

What changed. Empty sections of a topic page promised content "coming soon" with no date behind it — a promise the company could not keep or measure. It now says what is actually true.

How to check it Any topic hub with an unfilled cluster. The banned phrasing is now enforced by a check, so it cannot come back silently — it had already been rejected twice in design documents and was still live.

The measurement that re-aimed the content plan SEO-14 · 3pt

The retrospective

Held 02 September 2026, convened as a review board of six seats chaired by managing-editor — after the owner rejected a first pass the CEO had written alone. A synthesis is not a retrospective. Full document: docs/boardroom/meetings/2026-09-02-sprint-06-retrospective.md (606 lines); machine-readable at docs/sprints/sprint-06-retro.json.

1 · What we learned that was not written down

An instrument reports success about something it is not looking at. UI-16 counted six comfortable numbers in one evening on a single image slot — a green merge over a stale cache, a census that skipped its own rows, two type-check passes over files they excluded, an audit quiet because a fallback absorbed the defect, and a build diagnosis resting on a failure window with no closing edge. Not one was caught by a check going red. Every one was caught by somebody distrusting a number that looked fine.

The room corrected the CEO's framing of this: it is not "seven seats independently" but seven items across at most four seats, five of them one engineer. "Seven independent minds converging is a law of nature. One engineer noticing the same thing five times before dawn is a Tuesday."

3 · What we did twice

  • A carried figure was wrong for the fourth and fifth consecutive session. The article corpus moved 92 → 95 → 96 → 97 → 102 inside single items. The fix already works and is not better carrying: the definition of done says re-derive at run time, and every item that did got the right number.
  • Two seats measured the same element on the same day and shipped opposite answers — nothing made the collision visible until one of them merged. A scoping defect, and the CEO's to prevent.
  • Two items specified the same image name at different sizes on the same day, one command away from a year-long cache poisoning. Caught by a dry run's surprising count, not by any gate. Carried as UI-23.
  • The CEO reported a state reading as work three times in one night — most damningly inside its own answer to the owner's question about deferred work.

4 · What we nearly shipped, and what caught it

  • A religious restriction nobody issued. A government protocol clause was quoted accurately, with its number, checked against the source PDF — and it does not govern weddings. It was headed into structured data Google shows without the page around it. Caught by reading the document's own scope clause. "Not fabrication and not misquotation — a rule that nobody issued, wearing the authority of somebody who could have."
  • An invented citation while every sentence checked out. A summary attributed a real list to a page that does not contain it. "The summary did not invent the content, it invented the citation."
  • A 4.7 MB live regression on the image fallback path, invisible to every rule the repo owned.
  • A fabricated bug written into a source comment as fact — confident, specific and wrong. Killed by putting the code back and running the suite.

5 · Was everything actually shipped?

Yes, and every item was verified by the CEO against production or by running the item's own instrument — not on an agent's report. No item was self-marked done; all eleven were left open for verification, which is the behaviour the standard exists to produce. Two agents corrected the CEO's own figures at source and both were right.

One limitation stated rather than hidden: pnpm ui:gate:selftest could not be run on this machine — it hung twice with no output — so UI-19's 369-case self-test is not independently confirmed. The live rail measurement is, and that is the clause its definition of done turns on.

6 · What the sprint process itself got wrong

  • Nothing made the CONT-15 / UI-16 collision visible until a merge. The conflict rule covers two agents editing the same files; these edited different files that render the same pixel.
  • A board decision became a gate on arithmetic nobody wrote down. A twelve-point item was one interpretation away from being stopped by a number that could not be reproduced.
  • The CEO's own routing rule was wrong and two items re-derived it before anyone fixed the source.
  • The CEO reported a correlation as a cause while praising an agent for the rule that catches it.
  • Watchers died twice and a four-hour fleet stall went unnoticed. The owner found it by asking — exactly the failure Sprint 03 recorded and did not fix. Carried as PLAT-13.
  • The first retrospective was not one. The owner rejected it and was right; the skill is now gated.
