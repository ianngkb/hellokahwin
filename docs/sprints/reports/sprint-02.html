Read the velocity carefully

The tracker says 72 / 72 / 0. That is a flattering 100% and it is not the story.

The sprint was planned at 77 points across 20 items. SEO-04 (5pt) parked at its own sourcing gate and was moved to the backlog before close — and moving it out is exactly what turned 72 of 77 into 72 of 72.

Both numbers belong in the record: 19 of 20 items shipped, 72 of 77 planned points delivered, and the twentieth produced a decision not to build rather than a deliverable. Velocity is computed from item data and cannot be typed into — but it can still be flattered by moving an item, and that is worth knowing about the mechanism.

What shipped, item by item

Nineteen items. Each says what changed in plain terms and how to check it yourself.

RISK-04

Publishing an article now tells Google about it

3 pts

Until this sprint, publishing an article did nothing to inform Google. Five articles from the last sprint were sitting live and completely unknown to Google — not "waiting to be indexed", but never seen. Publishing now notifies Google as part of the same action.

How to check itOpen hellokahwin.com/sitemap.xml — it now lists 103 pages, up from 78. All four articles that were invisible left that state within eight hours, against a 48-hour target; two are already fully indexed.

RISK-06

Pages could be served from a year-old copy. Now the limit is 50 minutes.

3 pts

The site was allowed to serve a saved copy of any page for up to 365 days while it fetched a fresh one. Some visitors — possibly including Google — were being handed a page with the wrong title and no proper links. That window is now under an hour.

How to check itNothing to look at; it is a setting. The engineer proved the fault was real on 50 of 61 pages before fixing it — something the CEO had tried and failed to reproduce, because its own repeated visits kept refreshing the page it was trying to catch stale.

RISK-05

Something now watches for articles going invisible, and raises the alarm

2 pts

Last sprint we discovered articles were invisible to Google by chance. A daily check now inspects every page on the site and files a ticket automatically if any of them goes dark for more than three days.

How to check itIt has already fired for real — see issue #5, "1 sitemap URL(s) are dark to Google", filed and closed. It runs from the main branch, which is what a scheduled job needs to actually run — the thing that silently failed last sprint.

UX-01

On a phone, articles had no menu at all. Now they do.

3 pts

Someone arriving from Google onto an article on their phone got no logo, no menu, and no way to reach the rest of the site — just the article, and a footer thousands of pixels below. Since virtually all our search traffic is mobile and lands on articles, the site was effectively one page deep for almost every visitor.

How to check itOpen any article on your phone — the header with the section menu is there. Menu buttons also went from 32px to 44px, the size a thumb actually hits.

UX-02

Google can now see the individual halls inside our listicle

5 pts

Our best-performing page lists ten wedding halls by name, but to Google it was one undifferentiated page — there was no way to link to a single hall or for Google to recognise them as separate places. Each hall now has its own anchor and is described in the structured data search engines read.

How to check itOpen the dewan kahwin article. Twelve headings now carry their own link and eleven appear in a jump list; the underlying page names each hall as a place, where before it named none.

SEO-05

Five page titles rewritten, including one where Google was cutting off the price

2 pts

Titles were too long and Google was truncating them. On our most-visited page the words being cut were "RM5,000" — the single most compelling thing in the title. Five are rewritten, all now short enough to display in full and all carrying the year.

How to check itLook at the browser tab title on mas kahwin ikut negeri — it now reads the rate range rather than asking a question. The writer rejected two of the five pages the CEO nominated and picked better ones, on stronger evidence.

SEO-06

A section was advertising its own articles as "coming soon"

2 pts

The Hantaran section told readers two of its topics were still being written — while those articles existed, were published, and were ranking. They had simply been filed under the wrong section, so the page they belonged to never linked to them.

How to check itOpen the Hantaran section. It now has zero "akan datang tidak lama lagi" placeholders, down from three.

Cover photos now have to show what the article is about

3 pts

An article about gift trays was fronted by a photo of two guests' torsos with no tray in it. Nothing anywhere said a cover had to depict its subject, so nobody was wrong. Nineteen covers were re-chosen and the rule is now written where writers read it.

PLAT-05 · PLAT-06

Every document we own is now searchable in one place

13 pts

Four hundred-odd documents — briefs, specs, plans, logs, retrospectives — lived as files across two repositories and several folders, findable only if you already knew where they were. They are now searchable, readable and browsable in one place, organised the way the folders are.

PLAT-09

Everything in buddy is reachable from the sidebar, and a test keeps it that way

2 pts

The sprint board shipped last sprint and the only way to reach it was typing the address; the new document store would have inherited the same problem. All 23 pages now appear in the sidebar.

How to check itOpen buddy.ian.ng and look at the sidebar. The durable part is a test that reads the folder of pages and fails if any page lacks a menu entry — so a page added next month cannot quietly become unreachable.

PLAT-08

Changes can now be viewed on a private preview link before they go live

3 pts

There was no way to look at a change before it reached the public site — every preview build had been failing. Both projects now produce a working preview link for each proposed change.

How to check itThe preview links work for you and not for the CEO, because they sit behind your Vercel sign-in. That is deliberate — making them public would expose every unreleased change to anyone with the address. The honest position: "show me before you ship" now works for the owner only.

SEO-04

Venue pages — stopped at its own gate, and that was the right outcome

5 pts

The plan was a page per wedding venue. Its definition of done opened with a one-hour check — can we actually source real prices and capacities at scale? — that was explicitly allowed to stop the item. It fired. We cannot.

What that meansNothing was built. No pages, no entries, no invented capacities, and no quiet retreat to the weaker target the plan had already rejected — both named as forbidden in the brief, which is why neither happened. On a site whose whole claim is that its numbers carry sources, a directory of plausible fabrications would have been the worst possible outcome. Time, models & tokens

Empty for the second sprint running, and that is now a finding

The generator reads /buildit's ship-log to find a batch. A sprint does not write one, so it returns an empty card. Its literal output: "run-stats: no ship-log entry found; emitting empty card."

Why this is different from last timeSprint 01 reported this honestly and carried it forward. It was not acted on, and here it is again. By the retrospective's own rule — what did we do twice that we should never repeat — twice means the template is wrong, not the run. Either /startsprint writes the entry the tool expects, or the tool learns to key off a sprint. Reporting an empty card a third time is no longer acceptable, and that is now written into /endsprint rather than left as a note.

The retrospective

Every finding names the file it changed. A lesson with no target file evaporates.

Sprint 01's defining failure did not recur

Last sprint, three items were marked done without shipping and the owner found all three by asking. This sprint, every one of the 19 completed items was verified against production or against content on the default branch before being marked.

The ship check earned its place four separate times — PLAT-06 finished but six commits unpushed and seven ahead of main; SEO-05 finished with eleven site commits unpushed; four unpushed commits across two trees after the first wave; six unpushed docs commits on the final sweep. In each case the work was pushed and merged rather than marked done. PLAT-08 was reopened after marking itself done on a preview URL that returned a redirect rather than the page it claimed.

What broke instead was orchestration, and it broke four times in one night

A global sign-in event freezes the entire fleet and reports as success. Once on a usage limit that set a private 4:30am countdown, three times on login expiry. A /login in the orchestrator's session does not reach dispatched agents — each is a separate process holding its own credentials.

The dangerous part is not the freeze. It is that a stalled agent shows as IDLE/DONE — the exact string a finished agent shows. Two agents sat dead for two hours before anyone noticed, and at one point eight items read as complete when not one had finished. Marking those done would have been Sprint 01's failure with better paperwork.

The tell is clustering. Several agents going quiet inside one short window is not simultaneous completion.

Three agents were sent the same brief, and nothing errored

skillcentral/skills/hellokahwin/scripts/dispatch-agent.ps1

The dispatch script named its launcher after the agent, so three concurrent dispatches to the same agent all wrote one file — last write won, and two items came up running a third item's brief. Distinct handles, clean output, no error anywhere; only the post-dispatch brief audit caught it. This is very likely the true cause of Sprint 01's duplicate dispatch, which was diagnosed then as CEO error. That diagnosis produced a useful fix while the real mechanism sat in the launcher — a correct-looking fix for the wrong cause hides the real one.

The ship check called shipped work unshipped

skillcentral/skills/startsprint/SKILL.md

git merge-base --is-ancestor returns false forever for a squash-merged branch, because squashing makes a new commit and the branch tip never becomes an ancestor. A false negative is worse than no check — the response to "unshipped" is to ship it again. Verify by content on the default branch instead.

A rule written this sprint was used to kill one option and never applied to its replacement

docs/boardroom/ceo-memory.md

The SERP-ownership rule killed council halls because the council's own booking portal owns the results — then was not applied to the commercial venues that replaced them, which have their own brand sites above them for exactly the same reason. SEO-04's retrospective found this in the CEO's own rule. A rule that eliminates an option must be run against the replacement before the replacement is adopted, or it has only been used to justify a decision already made.

"Could not reproduce" is a statement about conditions, not about a page

docs/plans/…/aug-23-2026-workflow-content-production.md

The CEO could not reproduce the stale-page fault because its own repeated requests kept refreshing the very page it was trying to catch stale. The engineer, reading the source for a mechanism first, reproduced it on 50 of 61 pages on the first attempt. The same class of error nearly shipped in the new monitor, which initially treated a failed check as "absent" and would have reported clean over a shrinking denominator. Ask what the measurement cannot see, not only what it reports.

A gate that is allowed to kill the item is itself a deliverable

skillcentral/skills/hellokahwin/SKILL.md

SEO-04's sourcing gate fired and the item built nothing — no invented figures, no quiet downgrade to the rejected option, because the brief named both as forbidden. That shape is now the pattern for any item whose value depends on data we do not yet hold. A parked item costs 5 points; a fabricated one costs the thing the whole product is selling.

The team corrected the CEO four times, and every correction was buried in a deliverable

skillcentral/agents/projects/hellokahwin/Executive/ceo-hellokahwin.md

UX-01 could not reproduce the CEO's traffic figure and confirmed the shape instead of restating a number it had not measured. SEO-05 rejected two of five nominated pages on better evidence. CONT-07 retracted a false claim in its own log, unprompted. PLAT-06's agent refused to commit into a checkout another agent was using. None arrived as an escalation — they were inside work-done logs, target lists and commit messages, which means a skimmed report costs more than a slow one.

The CEO's own verification failed three times in the same shape

docs/boardroom/ceo-memory.md

Grepped for backlink when the file was document_links.sql. Guessed article slugs and got 404s when the agent's log had the real ones. Counted with grep -o and reported 2 where there was 1. Each first reading was confidently wrong. When a check returns a surprising absence, verify the check before believing the absence — enumerate what exists rather than asserting what does not.

What the sprint process got right, and should not be mistaken for luck

Ordering held. Risk ran first, and the publishing gate shipped before a single one of the 25 articles was written — so nothing published into the void Sprint 01 published into. Every declared dependency was respected: the re-filing landed before writers wrote into that cluster, the mobile fix released the site files before three UX items touched them, and the document store merged before the sidebar item added it to the menu.

Scope growth was flagged, never absorbed. 29 approved points became 77 in four documented steps, each from evidence produced after approval, each surfaced as it happened.

The owner was never the critical path except where they should have been — a pull request that would deploy the live public site at 2am was deliberately left open for them.
